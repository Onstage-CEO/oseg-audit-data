The satisfaction landscape

Across roughly six thousand verified reviews, service and staff lead this property's themes by a wide margin, followed by food and beverage and the pools and amenities, which sit close together. Cleanliness and rooms, value, and check-in wait times follow. Entertainment and atmosphere sit at the back of that list, named in about thirteen percent of reviews.

For these particular guests, that ordering matters. The people writing about The Phoenician are primarily evaluating attentive service and the dining and pool experience, and they fold entertainment into that judgment rather than rating it on its own. When live music shows up in a five-star review here, it tends to arrive attached to a meal at the steak house or an afternoon tea, not as a standalone night out. That is why the venue-level signal is the useful one: entertainment is being experienced inside the restaurants and lounges, so the room it plays in shapes how it lands.

Entertainment deep-dive

The headline belongs to The Phoenician Tavern. Its dueling pianos on Friday and Saturday nights are the property's signature entertainment, and guests respond warmly, with the Tavern's casual menu, wine list, and the boozy milkshakes it is known for all named in the same breath. Close behind is Thirsty Camel, where live music Thursday through Saturday turns drinks into an evening guests describe staying through until the music ends. Both venues carry solid ratings in their own right, and both anchor the bars and lounges category that accounts for the largest share of entertainment mentions here.

Against this property's own overall standard, in-venue entertainment rates about on par. The pattern underneath that average is what a general manager can act on. The bars and lounges, led by the Tavern and Thirsty Camel, are the clear top of the line, and the restaurants follow, with live music repeatedly cited as a complement to dinner and to afternoon tea. The pool decks and common areas are a much smaller share of mentions, and that is where the one consistent watch-item sits. The specific complaint is not about a performer or a show, it is about piped background music carried through speakers across the grounds and into spaces guests expected to be quiet. So the property has live entertainment that guests seek out in the right rooms, and ambient sound that a minority of guests experience as intrusive in the wrong ones. Those are two different things, and treating them separately is the point.

The guest voice

Most guests who mention entertainment here speak well of it, and the live music at the Tavern, at Thirsty Camel, and in the dining rooms is the recurring reason. At the same time, a meaningful minority did not have that experience: of the entertainment-related mentions in this review set, a number of guests rated their experience one or two stars. The sharpest of those critical notes is not about the live acts at all, it is about amplified background music across the pools and common areas being too loud and too constant. That honest split, strong venue entertainment alongside a real ambient-sound irritant, is the most useful thing the guest voice tells you.

The opportunity

The most direct gain is to extend what already works. The dueling pianos at The Phoenician Tavern and the live music at Thirsty Camel are the two formats guests name without prompting, and they are concentrated on Thursday through Saturday. There is room to lengthen those windows or add complementary live sets on the quieter nights so the evening program reads as consistent rather than weekend-only, while keeping the mellow, lounge-led tone that guests specifically praise.

The second move addresses the watch-item directly. The complaints about amplified background music in the pools and common areas point to sound design rather than to live performance. Reviewing speaker placement and volume across the grounds, and protecting some seating areas as genuinely quiet, would remove the single most repeated entertainment irritant without touching the live program. Onstage can help on both fronts at once, strengthening the live calendar at the named venues and shaping the ambient sound across the common areas as one coordinated effort, so the evening atmosphere matches the standard guests already hold this property to in service and dining.

Methodology

Coverage statement: This report analyzes 6,010 captured public guest reviews. Platform-level public totals were not refreshed for every source in the latest capture status, so this report does not present a summed public-review universe. The reviews analyzed span Oct 2002 to Jun 2026. The figures below are based on the captured review set, not an assertion that every public review on every platform was downloaded. Where a source's captured count is below its public total, the difference is typically rating-only entries (a star score with no written text), which carry no analyzable content and are not part of the figures.

This report draws on roughly six thousand verified public guest reviews of The Phoenician Resort and Spa across Booking.com, TripAdvisor, Expedia, and Google, the large majority in English. Entertainment findings are based on the reviews that actually mention in-venue entertainment and atmosphere, with venue-level ratings drawn from guest reviews tied to The Phoenician Tavern and Thirsty Camel. We supplemented the review reading with a scan of public forums, social posts, and travel editorial to confirm the named venues and program patterns. No ratings, counts, or quotes were estimated or invented.
