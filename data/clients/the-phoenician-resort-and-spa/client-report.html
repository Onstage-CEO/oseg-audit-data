The resort-wide figures above are computed from 282 property-level dated public reviews spanning Jul 2025 to Jun 2026. The entertainment findings additionally draw on 6,699 outlet-level OpenTable venue reviews; outlet ratings are venue-specific and are not blended into the resort-wide score.

The clearest signal in your review set is a split: The Phoenician Resort and Spa earns broad praise for service and its pool and amenities, yet the entertainment guests do encounter rates below the property's own high standard. On a four-star aggregate across 282 verified reviews, in-venue entertainment sits at a noticeably lower mark, and it appears in only about one in ten reviews. That is not a crisis, and it is not the property's defining story. It is a focused, high-leverage gap: the resort excels widely, and entertainment is the one place where guest experience trails what these same guests expect from everything else here.

The headline entertainment moment guests actually talk about is the dueling pianos at The Phoenician Tavern on Saturday nights. It lands well, and it is the strongest entertainment story you have. Alongside it, live music on weekend evenings at Thirsty Camel draws steady, warm mentions. Both venues carry strong standalone ratings, which makes the gap more interesting: when the entertainment is present and named, guests respond to it.

The watch-item is the thinness and inconsistency of that entertainment relative to how these guests rate the rest of the resort. Of the small set of guests who spoke to entertainment directly, a meaningful number rated the experience poorly. When your service and pool scores set the bar this high, an entertainment layer that only occasionally reaches that level becomes the visible seam. This report shows where that seam sits. The on-site diagnostic is where the validated, costed roadmap is delivered.

The satisfaction landscape

The conversation at this property is led by service and staff, which appears in more than half of all reviews, followed by the pool and amenities and then food and beverage. Cleanliness and room, value, and check-in wait times fill out the middle of the mix. Entertainment and atmosphere sit at the bottom of the named themes, in roughly one in ten reviews. For these guests, entertainment is a smaller and more specific part of the stay than the service they praise or the pool they return for. That placement matters because it tells you where the leverage is: entertainment is not carrying the property's reputation, so it does not need to. It needs to stop trailing the standard the rest of the resort has already set. When a guest who came for the pool and the service also happens to catch entertainment, the comparison is unforgiving, because everything else here is rated so highly.

Entertainment deep-dive

The signature entertainment moment here is the dueling pianos at The Phoenician Tavern on Saturday nights. In its own reviews the Tavern rates strongly, and guests single out the Saturday piano night as genuine fun, paired with a casual menu, the boozy milkshakes it is known for, and what one guest called perhaps the best wine list of any bar or restaurant on the property. That is a real, specific draw, and it is the anchor of your entertainment story.

The second named venue is Thirsty Camel, which runs live music on weekend evenings. Guests describe a mellow ambiance and, on the nights the music is on, staying until the music ends. It too carries a strong standalone rating. Between these two, the pattern is consistent: when entertainment is present and tied to a named room, guests respond warmly and stay longer.

The difficulty is that this warmth is concentrated and occasional, while the property's overall bar is high and constant. Across the review set, in-venue entertainment shows up in the bars and lounges most often, then restaurants, then the pool decks and common areas, and the average guests give it sits below the resort's overall rating. Among the guests who spoke to entertainment directly, sentiment splits: a similar number of strong positive mentions and low-scoring ones. So the working parts are clear and named. The watch-item is equally clear: entertainment does not yet meet, with any consistency, the standard your service and pool scores have set. That is the specific gap, and it is grounded in what guests at these venues are actually saying.

The guest voice

The honest read is that entertainment here is not widely discussed, and when it is, guests fall on both sides. Most who mention it praise it, the Saturday dueling pianos at The Phoenician Tavern and the weekend live music at Thirsty Camel both draw warm, specific comments. At the same time, of the small set of guests who spoke to entertainment directly, four rated their experience 1 or 2 stars. That real number, not an inflated read, is the honest case for attention. One low-scoring guest even noted the music itself was great while the complaint sat elsewhere, which is a useful reminder that the entertainment can land even when the visit does not.

The opportunity

The single most valuable and reachable opportunity is the gap between how highly guests rate The Phoenician overall and how they rate the entertainment they actually encounter. You have two proven anchors, the Saturday dueling pianos at The Phoenician Tavern and the weekend live music at Thirsty Camel, and both perform well on their own reviews. But entertainment appears in only about one in ten stays, its average sits below the property's overall standard, and the guests who do speak to it split evenly between strong praise and low scores. That inconsistency is the pain. For a resort whose reputation rests on uniformly high service and a beloved pool, an entertainment layer that only sometimes reaches that level is the visible seam, the one part of the experience that does not match the rest.

This matters to your scores because entertainment is the theme with the most room to move: it is not dragging the property down, but it is the clearest place where guest experience trails the bar you have already set everywhere else. Closing that gap lifts the one area currently under-delivering against your own standard, without touching what is already working. It is a focused, fixable problem, and the signal in your reviews shows exactly where it lives. Naming the solution is the work of the on-site diagnostic, which is where the validated, costed roadmap is delivered.

Methodology

Coverage statement: This report analyzes 282 captured property-level public guest reviews. Across sources that returned a public total, 4,241 public reviews were identified. The reviews analyzed span Jul 2025 to Jun 2026. The entertainment findings additionally draw on a separate outlet-level corpus of 6,699 OpenTable venue reviews (J&G Steakhouse 3,436, Afternoon Tea – The Phoenician 1,678, Mowry & Cotton 1,162, The Phoenician Tavern 423), for a combined property-plus-OpenTable base of 6,981 records; those outlet ratings are venue-specific and are not blended into the resort-wide score. The figures below are based on the captured review set, not an assertion that every public review on every platform was downloaded. Where a source's captured count is below its public total, the difference is typically rating-only entries (a star score with no written text), which carry no analyzable content and are not part of the figures.

This report is built only from verified public guest reviews for The Phoenician Resort and Spa, 282 dated reviews across Booking.com, TripAdvisor, Expedia, and Google, supported by venue-level review counts for named outlets and a scan of open-web sources including travel editorial and forums. We attribute entertainment findings only to the specific named venues and acts that run them, and we separate live-entertainment sentiment from unrelated operational or pricing comments. Where the signal is thin, we say so plainly rather than overstate it.
