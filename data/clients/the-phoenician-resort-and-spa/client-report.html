The defining entertainment fact at The Phoenician is that two named venues carry the live program, and they carry it well: the Saturday dueling pianos at The Phoenician Tavern, and the Thursday-through-Saturday live music at Thirsty Camel. Across roughly six thousand verified reviews, guests who write about in-venue entertainment rate it almost exactly in line with how they rate the resort overall. Entertainment is not a weak spot here, and it is not a runaway strength either. It sits on par with this property's standard, which means the work is precise rather than broad: find which formats pull above the line and protect them.

Entertainment appears in about one in eight reviews, behind the themes that define this resort for most guests: service and staff, food and beverage, and the pools and amenities. So the live program is one meaningful thread in a larger story, and it most often shows up woven into a dining or lounge visit rather than as a standalone night out.

The honest read is that the great majority of entertainment mentions are positive, with a strong count of five-star write-ups, while a small number of guests rated their entertainment experience poorly. The opportunity is to lift the consistency of the program at the bars and lounges, where most of the entertainment conversation lives, rather than to fix anything broken.

The satisfaction landscape

Reading the actual theme mix for The Phoenician, service and staff lead by a wide margin and appear in nearly two-thirds of reviews, with food and beverage and the pools and amenities close behind one another. Cleanliness and room, value, and check-in follow. Entertainment and atmosphere sits at the back of that list at roughly thirteen percent. For these guests, that placement matters: people come for the service and the pools, and they encounter the live program in the course of a meal or a drink. That is why the venues where entertainment lives, the bars and lounges and the restaurants, are the right place to focus. The entertainment conversation is not separate from the food and beverage conversation here; it is part of it.

Entertainment deep-dive

The headline entertainment story at The Phoenician is the Saturday dueling pianos at The Phoenician Tavern. Across its review set the Tavern holds a solid rating, and the guest praise is specific: people describe a Saturday night of dueling pianos as a lot of fun, paired with a casual menu, the boozie milkshakes the room is known for, and what one guest called the best wine list of any bar or restaurant on the property. That is a signature format guests remember and name. The second pillar is Thirsty Camel, with live music Thursday through Saturday; guests describe a mellow ambiance where they came in for drinks and stayed until the music ended, and they single out the attentive staff.

Against this property's own overall standard, in-venue entertainment rates about on par. The pattern underneath that average is clear: the bars and lounges are where most of the entertainment conversation happens, well ahead of the restaurants, with the pool decks and common areas a distant third. The two named venues, the Tavern and Thirsty Camel, are doing the heavy lifting and doing it at a high level. What is working is the pairing of a defined live format with strong food, beverage, and service. The watch-item is consistency: because entertainment here is woven into the bar and lounge experience rather than a standalone draw, an off night reads to a guest as a flat lounge visit, not a missed show. The small group of low-star entertainment mentions is where that inconsistency shows up.

The guest voice

The guest voice on entertainment at The Phoenician is strongly positive but not unanimous, and both sides deserve to be named plainly. Most guests who mention the live program praise it, and a large share of those are five-star write-ups tied to the dueling pianos at the Tavern and the live music at Thirsty Camel. At the same time, of the guests who wrote about their entertainment experience, 33 rated it 1 or 2 stars. That real number, not an inflated reading of it, is the honest case for tightening consistency. The praise quotes and the critical quotes are shown separately below.

The opportunity

The clearest place for Onstage to add value at The Phoenician is consistency across the bars and lounges, where most of the entertainment conversation already happens. The Phoenician Tavern has a signature in its Saturday dueling pianos, and Thirsty Camel has a dependable Thursday-through-Saturday live music rhythm; the goal is to make every night a guest walks in feel as defined as the nights guests currently write home about. That means a steady booking standard and a known format guests can count on, so the live program reinforces the service and food strengths that already lead this property rather than depending on which night a guest happens to visit.

Because entertainment here is part of the dining and lounge experience, the opportunity is also to give the restaurants a lighter, repeatable live element that matches the Tavern's draw without competing with it. The resort's seasonal activations, such as its Halloween celebration, point to an audience that responds to a programmed moment; a consistent year-round standard would extend that beyond the calendar peaks. Onstage would manage this as one partner, so the program stays even across venues and nights.

A note on sound. The most frequent sound-related complaint in these reviews concerns piped background music in the pool and common areas, not the live performances. That is a house-audio setting, addressed separately from the live entertainment assessed in this report; the live program is not the source.

Methodology

Coverage statement: This report analyzes 6,010 captured public guest reviews. Platform-level public totals were not refreshed for every source in the latest capture status, so this report does not present a summed public-review universe. The reviews analyzed span Oct 2002 to Jun 2026. The figures below are based on the captured review set, not an assertion that every public review on every platform was downloaded. Where a source's captured count is below its public total, the difference is typically rating-only entries (a star score with no written text), which carry no analyzable content and are not part of the figures.

This report draws on roughly six thousand verified public guest reviews of The Phoenician Resort and Spa across Booking.com, TripAdvisor, Expedia, and Google, the large majority in English, spanning the full dated history of the review set. Theme shares, venue mention counts, and entertainment ratings are computed from those reviews; venue-level findings reflect the named venues that appear in the guest record. Open-web editorial and forum signal was reviewed only to confirm venue and program context, never to assert facts not present in the reviews themselves.
