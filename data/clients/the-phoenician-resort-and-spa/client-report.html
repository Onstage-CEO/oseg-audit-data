The satisfaction landscape

What guests talk about at The Phoenician is led, by a wide margin, by service and staff, which appears in nearly two-thirds of reviews. Food and beverage and the pools and amenities follow closely, each in just under half of reviews, and those three themes define how guests describe the property. Cleanliness and rooms, value, and check-in wait times sit in the middle. Entertainment and atmosphere comes in last among the tracked themes at about 13 percent. That placement is not a weakness, it reflects a resort where the grounds, the pools, the spa, the golf, and the dining are the headline draws. Entertainment here is experienced mostly inside the dining and bar venues, not as a standalone draw, which is exactly why the named bars matter more than any general evening program.

Entertainment deep-dive

The headline entertainment story is the Saturday dueling pianos at The Phoenician Tavern. Guests single it out, and the Tavern's signature boozy milkshakes and wine list show up in the same breath, so the music is landing as part of a complete evening rather than a sideshow. The Tavern carries a solid rating across a healthy volume of reviews. The Thirsty Camel is the second anchor, with live music Thursday through Saturday and a mellow, date-night ambiance that guests describe as a reason to stay later than planned. It rates slightly above the Tavern on its own review volume.

Across the wider review set, in-venue entertainment rates about on par with the property's overall standard. The pattern is that entertainment reads best when it is tied to a specific venue with a known schedule. Bars and lounges draw the most entertainment mentions by a clear margin, restaurants are second, and live music in the dining rooms and lounge is consistently mentioned as a positive, including at high tea and the steak house. The pool decks and common areas draw far fewer mentions, and what appears there is more mixed.

The specific watch-item lives in those common areas. Rather than live performance, the issue is ambient piped music by the pools and in the restrooms, which a small number of guests found loud and hard to escape. That is a different lever from the live bar programming and should be managed separately, because the venues are working while the background audio is the part that occasionally pulls below the line.

At the same time, an honest reading names the other side: while most guests praise the entertainment, 34 rated their entertainment experience 1 or 2 stars. The critical voice is narrow and consistent, centered on ambient music volume in the pools and common areas rather than the live programming itself. The opportunity

The clearest opportunity is to make the Saturday dueling pianos at The Phoenician Tavern and the Thursday-to-Saturday live music at the Thirsty Camel easier to find before guests arrive. Today these land as happy surprises for guests who stumble onto the right night, which means the property is not getting full credit for programming it already runs. Publishing the weekly schedule where guests plan their evenings would convert chance encounters into booked ones.

Second, the live music that guests praise inside the restaurants, the steak house, the lounge, and at high tea is a proven format worth protecting and extending to more nights, since it consistently reads as a treat rather than noise.

Third, treat the common-area audio as its own item. The poolside and restroom music complaints are about volume and placement, not about live performance, so resolving them is a quiet, low-cost fix that removes the main drag on the entertainment theme. Onstage can work as one partner across the Tavern, the Thirsty Camel, and the dining rooms to keep the live formats consistent and well scheduled.

Methodology

Coverage statement: This report analyzes 6,010 captured public guest reviews. Platform-level public totals were not refreshed for every source in the latest capture status, so this report does not present a summed public-review universe. The reviews analyzed span Oct 2002 to Jun 2026. The figures below are based on the captured review set, not an assertion that every public review on every platform was downloaded. Where a source's captured count is below its public total, the difference is typically rating-only entries (a star score with no written text), which carry no analyzable content and are not part of the figures.

This report draws on 6,010 verified public guest reviews captured from Booking.com, TripAdvisor, Expedia, and Google, of which 417 touch on entertainment and atmosphere. Venue-level findings are built only from reviews that name The Phoenician Tavern, the Thirsty Camel, and the property's restaurants and common areas. Open-web scanning of forums, social platforms, and travel editorial was used to confirm context, not to assert figures. No benchmark, revenue, or pricing figure is implied beyond what the reviews themselves state.
