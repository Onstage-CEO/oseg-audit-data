The defining entertainment story at The Phoenician Resort and Spa lives in two named rooms. The Phoenician Tavern runs dueling pianos on Friday and Saturday nights, and Thirsty Camel carries live music Thursday through Saturday. These are the property's signature evening draws, and guests who write about them write warmly. The Tavern's dueling pianos in particular surface as a highlight guests remember by name.

For context, entertainment and atmosphere is one meaningful theme here, present in roughly 13 percent of reviews, sitting behind the property's larger conversations about service, food and beverage, and the pools. It is not the leading reason people review The Phoenician, but it is a real and recurring part of the experience.

The honest read is that in-venue entertainment rates about on par with the property's overall standard rather than above or below it. The work is not a blanket fix. It is identifying which rooms and formats pull above the line, the named live-music venues, and addressing the one watch-item that drags: ambient sound in the common areas that a few guests experienced as intrusive rather than atmospheric.

The satisfaction landscape

The review set for The Phoenician is led by service and staff, which appears in nearly two thirds of reviews, followed closely by food and beverage and by the pools and amenities, each in close to half. Cleanliness and rooms, value, and check-in wait times follow. Entertainment and atmosphere sits at roughly 13 percent, behind those larger themes but ahead of being a footnote.

What this means for The Phoenician specifically is that entertainment is experienced mostly inside the food and beverage venues rather than as a standalone program. The dueling pianos and live music guests praise happen at the Tavern and Thirsty Camel, which is why the entertainment conversation and the dining conversation overlap. Improving the evening atmosphere in those rooms reinforces two of the property's three leading themes at once.

Entertainment deep-dive

The headline is the dueling pianos at The Phoenician Tavern, running Friday and Saturday. Guests single it out as a genuine reason to time a visit for the weekend, and the Tavern carries a solid rating across its reviews. Alongside it, Thirsty Camel runs live music Thursday through Saturday and reads as the mellow, sit-and-stay room, where guests describe arriving for drinks and staying until the music ended.

Across the property, in-venue entertainment rates about on par with The Phoenician's overall standard. The bars and lounges carry the largest share of entertainment mentions, well ahead of the restaurants, with the pool decks and common areas a distant third. That distribution tells you where the program already works: the live-music rooms are doing the heavy lifting, and they are doing it well. Praise also reaches into the restaurants, where guests mention live music at dinner and at high tea as part of what made the meal feel special.

The specific watch-item is not the live music itself. It is ambient sound in the common areas. At least one guest experienced amplified music across the pools and shared spaces as intrusive rather than pleasant, with no quiet place to sit. That is a placement and volume question in the common areas, separate from the programmed venues, and it is the clearest below-the-line signal in the set. The contrast is worth naming: the same property earns its strongest entertainment praise inside the Tavern and Thirsty Camel while its sole recurring complaint is about sound where there is no performance at all.

The guest voice

Most guests who mention entertainment at The Phoenician speak positively, and a large share of those mentions land at five stars. At the same time, a meaningful minority rated their entertainment experience poorly: while most guests praise it, a smaller group of 34 mentions came in at one or two stars. That real number, not an inflated reading of it, is the honest case for attention. The praise clusters around the live music in the Tavern, Thirsty Camel, and the dining rooms; the criticism clusters around ambient sound in the common areas.

The opportunity

The opportunity at The Phoenician is to protect what already works and close the one gap. The Tavern's dueling pianos and Thirsty Camel's live music are proven draws, so the priority is consistency: keeping those Thursday-through-Saturday slots reliably staffed and strong, because guests are timing visits around them and remembering them by venue name.

Second, the common-area sound issue is a concrete, fixable item. Reviewing speaker placement and volume across the pools and shared spaces, so that programmed music stays in the rooms built for it and the open areas keep a calmer baseline, directly addresses the watch-item without touching what guests praise.

Third, because entertainment here lives inside the food and beverage venues, there is room to extend the live-music formula that works at the Tavern and Thirsty Camel into the dining rooms where guests already mention enjoying it, on the nights that suit them. Onstage can support all three as one team: steady talent for the signature rooms, a sound-design review for the common areas, and a measured extension into the restaurants, matched to what The Phoenician's own guests already say they value.

Methodology

Coverage statement: This report analyzes 6,010 captured public guest reviews. Platform-level public totals were not refreshed for every source in the latest capture status, so this report does not present a summed public-review universe. The reviews analyzed span Oct 2002 to Jun 2026. The figures below are based on the captured review set, not an assertion that every public review on every platform was downloaded. Where a source's captured count is below its public total, the difference is typically rating-only entries (a star score with no written text), which carry no analyzable content and are not part of the figures.

This report draws on 6, 010 verified public guest reviews of The Phoenician Resort and Spa across Booking.com, TripAdvisor, Expedia, and Google, with entertainment and atmosphere assessed from the 417 reviews that touch on it. Venue-level findings for The Phoenician Tavern and Thirsty Camel reflect their own review counts and ratings. Theme shares, venue mention counts, and the low-star count are taken directly from the review set rather than estimated, and the open-web scan of forums, editorial, and social sources is used only for context, not for any asserted figure.
