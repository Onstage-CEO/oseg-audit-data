The single most defining entertainment fact at The Phoenician Resort and Spa is the dueling pianos at The Phoenician Tavern on Friday and Saturday nights. Guests who write about that room call out the format specifically and credit it for making the venue feel special, even as the Tavern itself carries a rating below the property's overall standard. Across roughly 2, 400 verified public reviews, service and staff dominate the conversation, with food and beverage and the pool and amenities close behind. Entertainment and atmosphere appear in about 7 percent of reviews. That makes it a focused, high-leverage area rather than the property's main story.

Within that focused slice, in-venue entertainment rates about on par with the resort's overall standard. The picture is not a blanket pass or fail. Specific rooms and formats pull above the line, and at least one recurring complaint pulls below it. The job is to protect what already works and address the narrow set of friction points, not to overhaul a program guests largely enjoy.

The satisfaction landscape

Reading the actual theme mix for this property, service and staff lead by a wide margin and anchor nearly half of all reviews. Food and beverage and the pool and amenities form the second tier of what guests talk about. Cleanliness and room, value and price, and check-in and wait follow. Entertainment and atmosphere sit at the bottom of the named themes by volume, which is the honest placement for a resort where guests come first for service and dining.

What matters for these specific guests is that entertainment, when it does come up, tends to ride alongside food and beverage rather than standing on its own. The reviews that mention live music almost always mention a restaurant, a lounge, or the lobby in the same breath. So the leverage point is clear: entertainment here is not a separate attraction guests seek out, it is the atmosphere layer on top of the dining and bar experiences that already drive the property's reputation.

Entertainment deep-dive

The headline entertainment story is the dueling pianos at The Phoenician Tavern, running Friday and Saturday evenings. Guests describe it as a fun, casual, friendly atmosphere and advise others to reserve a table because the room fills up. That is a real, repeatable draw with a clear format and a clear schedule. Notably, the Tavern's own venue rating runs below the resort's overall standard, which tells us the entertainment is one of the stronger reasons people enjoy that room rather than a reflection of the room's broader scores.

Beyond the Tavern, the named venues split fairly evenly across restaurants and bars and lounges, with a smaller set of mentions tied to the pool decks and common areas. The Thirsty Camel lounge draws specific praise for live music, and the lobby happy hour with music shows up as a positive atmosphere note. Piano music at afternoon English tea is referenced as part of the ambiance as well. The pattern is consistent: live music inside the food and beverage venues is what guests notice and reward.

Against this property's own standard, in-venue entertainment rates about on par overall, with the bright spots clustered in the lounge and lobby music and the weekend pianos. The specific watch-item is acoustics and placement rather than the programming itself. One guest in a top suite reported noise carrying up from the steakhouse below late into the evening, and a lobby review praised the music while noting the food and drink in that space fell short. The entertainment is landing. The room-level fit, sound bleed into guest rooms and the food and beverage offer around the music, is where the friction sits.

The guest voice

The honest read is that most guests who mention entertainment praise it, and a meaningful minority did not. Of the entertainment mentions in this set, seven guests rated their entertainment experience at 1 or 2 stars, against a much larger group of five-star mentions. That real number is the case for refinement, not alarm. The critical voices are specific and actionable, pointing to price perception at the bar and to music carrying into guest rooms, while the praise consistently names the live music in the lounges, the lobby, and the weekend pianos.

The opportunity

The clearest place Onstage elevates this property is the weekend dueling pianos at The Phoenician Tavern. It already works and already has a following, so the move is to protect it and extend it: consistent talent quality on Friday and Saturday, and a look at whether a complementary live-music night earlier in the week could lift the Tavern's overall scores, which currently trail the resort average.

Second, the lounge and lobby music is a proven atmosphere driver. The Thirsty Camel lounge live music and the lobby happy hour both surface as positives. Standardizing the quality and scheduling of that music across these spaces turns scattered good moments into a reliable, named part of the guest experience.

Third, address the acoustics watch-item directly. The complaint about music from the steakhouse reaching a top suite is a placement and sound-management issue, not a programming one. Coordinating set times, volume, and end times with the rooms directly above and around live-music venues protects the guests paying the most while keeping the entertainment that other guests value. One team managing both the talent and the in-room fit keeps these decisions aligned.
