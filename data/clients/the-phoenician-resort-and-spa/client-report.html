The most distinctive entertainment fact about The Phoenician Resort and Spa is that its signature programming lives inside two named bar venues rather than across the property: the dueling pianos at The Phoenician Tavern on Friday and Saturday nights, and the live music at Thirsty Camel from Thursday through Saturday. These are the moments guests single out by name when they write about atmosphere, and they sit at the center of how entertainment reads here.

Entertainment is one meaningful theme among several at this property, appearing in roughly 13 percent of reviews. It rates about on par with the resort's overall standard, so the useful work is not a blanket verdict but identifying which formats pull above the line and which sit below it. The in-venue programming concentrated in bars and lounges is the clear strength. The watch-item is narrower and specific: ambient music piped into the pools and common areas, where at least one guest described being surrounded by speakers with no quiet place to sit.

For a property whose reputation is built on service, food, and the pools, entertainment is not the headline, but the named bar programming is a real asset that a small number of guests have flagged unevenly.

The satisfaction landscape

Across roughly 6, 000 reviews from Booking.com, TripAdvisor, Expedia, and Google, service and staff lead this property's themes by a wide margin, followed closely by food and beverage and by the pools and amenities, which are mentioned at nearly the same rate. Cleanliness and rooms, value, and check-in wait times follow. Entertainment and atmosphere sits at the end of that list, in about 13 percent of reviews.

That placement matters for how the GM should read it. At The Phoenician, guests come for the service and the pools, and they talk about entertainment mostly as it surfaces inside the dining and bar experience, high tea with live music, the steak house with live music, the lounge. Entertainment here is woven into food and beverage rather than standing apart from it, which is why the bar venues carry the entertainment story.

Entertainment deep-dive

The headline entertainment story is the dueling pianos at The Phoenician Tavern, the property's signature evening format, running Friday and Saturday. The Tavern carries a solid guest rating across its review set, and the standout guest account ties the pianos to the broader appeal of the room, a casual menu that feels like good value, the boozy milkshakes the venue is known for, and what that guest called the best wine list of any bar or restaurant on the property. This is the clearest case where in-venue entertainment lifts the whole visit.

Close behind is Thirsty Camel, with live music Thursday through Saturday. It rates similarly well, and guests describe a mellow ambiance where the music is reason enough to stay through the evening. Together these two venues define the pattern: entertainment works best at this property when it is live, scheduled, and inside a bar or lounge with attentive service around it.

Set against the resort's own overall standard, in-venue entertainment rates about on par, not a runaway leader and not a drag. Bars and lounges account for most entertainment mentions, with restaurants a meaningful second, which fits the recurring guest references to live music at dinner and at high tea. The pools and common areas draw far fewer entertainment mentions, and that is where the one specific watch-item lives: ambient music pushed through speakers across the pool decks and even into restrooms, which one guest experienced as inescapable rather than welcome. The contrast is worth holding onto. The same property where live music in a lounge is called a treat is also where piped background audio in the common areas has drawn a sharp complaint.

The guest voice

Most guests who mention entertainment at The Phoenician speak well of it, and the in-venue programming carries a strong share of five-star mentions. At the same time, an honest read names both sides: while most guests praise it, 34 guests rated their entertainment experience at the low end of the scale. The praise clusters around live music in the lounges, at the steak house, and at high tea, and around the dueling pianos at the Tavern. The criticism is more specific than general, centered on ambient speaker music in the pools and common areas rather than on the live programming itself.

The opportunity

The most actionable opening is the gap between the two settings. The live, scheduled programming at The Phoenician Tavern and Thirsty Camel is working and rates with the property's overall standard, while the piped background audio across the pool decks and common areas is the one place guests have pushed back. A first step a GM could act on is treating the common-area soundscape with the same intent as the booked venues: defining where ambient audio belongs, where quiet seating is protected, and matching volume and zoning to how guests actually use those spaces.

Second, the entertainment that lands here is consistently tied to food and beverage, high tea, the steak house, the lounge, so the strength is best extended by deepening the live-music presence already inside those rooms rather than adding formats disconnected from dining. The Tavern's dueling pianos run only Friday and Saturday and Thirsty Camel's live music Thursday through Saturday; understanding demand earlier in the week is a concrete question worth testing. Onstage can help structure the in-venue calendar and the common-area sound plan as one program, with one team accountable for both.

Methodology

Coverage statement: This report analyzes 6,010 captured public guest reviews. Platform-level public totals were not refreshed for every source in the latest capture status, so this report does not present a summed public-review universe. The reviews analyzed span Oct 2002 to Jun 2026. The figures below are based on the captured review set, not an assertion that every public review on every platform was downloaded. Where a source's captured count is below its public total, the difference is typically rating-only entries (a star score with no written text), which carry no analyzable content and are not part of the figures.

This report draws on roughly 6, 000 verified public guest reviews of The Phoenician Resort and Spa across Booking.com, TripAdvisor, Expedia, and Google, the large majority in English, alongside a scan of open-web sources including travel forums, Yelp, and social platforms. Entertainment findings rest on the subset of reviews that reference atmosphere and in-venue programming, with venue-level reads tied to the named bars and restaurants where guests describe those experiences.
