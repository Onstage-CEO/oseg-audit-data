The defining entertainment story at The Phoenician Resort and Spa is venue-specific, not property-wide. Two named rooms carry the live program. The Phoenician Tavern runs dueling pianos on Friday and Saturday nights, and Thirsty Camel runs live music Thursday through Saturday. Both sit just below the resort's overall guest rating, which tells you the entertainment here is solid and well-liked rather than a standout that pulls the whole property up.

Entertainment is one meaningful theme among several, appearing in about 13 percent of reviews. In-venue entertainment rates about on par with the resort's overall standard, so the right read is not a blanket verdict but a search for the specific rooms and formats that sit above or below the line. The bars and lounges generate the most entertainment conversation by a wide margin, with the restaurants second and the pool decks and common areas a distant third.

One watch-item is worth a GM's attention. Ambient music in the open common areas and pools is a recurring point of friction for a minority of guests, separate from the booked live program in the venues. The live rooms earn praise. The piped background sound in shared spaces does not always land the same way.

Looking at the before and after Onstage view (October 2017), entertainment's share of conversation has settled lower in the more recent period while the overall rating has held essentially flat. The program is steady; the opportunity is to make the live rooms a bigger part of why guests talk about this resort.

The satisfaction landscape

The conversation at The Phoenician is led by service and staff, which appears in nearly two-thirds of reviews, followed closely by food and beverage and by the pool and amenities, each in close to half. Cleanliness and rooms, value, and check-in wait times sit in the middle. Entertainment and atmosphere lands at about 13 percent of reviews.

For these guests, that placement matters. The Phoenician is read first as a service-and-amenity resort, with multiple pools, a spa, golf, and a long activity list that guests cite directly. Entertainment is not the lead reason people come, but it threads through the themes that are leading: live music shows up inside the food and beverage conversation, in the lounges, and at high tea. That overlap is the practical point. Entertainment here rises or falls inside the venues guests already rate highly, which is where a stronger live program does the most work.

Entertainment deep-dive

The headline entertainment at this resort is the dueling pianos at The Phoenician Tavern on Friday and Saturday nights. Guests rate the Tavern just over four stars, and the review set shows the dueling-pianos night landing as a genuine highlight, paired with the casual menu, the boozy milkshakes the room is known for, and what one guest called the best wine list on the property. This is the signature entertainment story and it is working.

The second live room is Thirsty Camel, with live music Thursday through Saturday and a similar rating just above four stars. Guests describe a mellow ambiance and attentive staff, with people staying through the end of the set. Both rooms sit a little below the resort's overall standard, which places in-venue entertainment about on par with the property rather than ahead of it. These two bars and lounges, plus live music inside the restaurants, are where the entertainment conversation actually happens, and the bars and lounges drive far more of it than any other space.

What is working is the booked live music in the venues: the dueling pianos, the lounge sets, and live music tied to dining and to high tea all earn five-star mentions. The specific watch-item is different in kind. It is the ambient background music in the open common areas and around the pools, where a minority of guests describe the piped sound in shared spaces and even restrooms as intrusive. That is a programming-and-placement issue in the common areas, not a problem with the live rooms, and it is worth separating cleanly so the strong live program does not get blamed for the background-audio friction.

The guest voice

Most guests who mention entertainment speak well of it, and the live rooms draw consistent praise: dueling pianos at the Tavern, live music at Thirsty Camel, and music tied to dining and high tea. At the same time, a meaningful minority pushed back. While most guests praise the entertainment, 34 rated their entertainment experience at one or two stars, and the sharpest of those complaints are about piped background music in the pools and common areas rather than the booked performances. That real number is the honest case for attention, and it points at a specific, fixable place rather than at the live program as a whole.

The opportunity

There are two concrete moves here. First, lean into the rooms that already work. The dueling pianos at The Phoenician Tavern and live music at Thirsty Camel both rate just below the resort's overall standard, which means small, consistent improvements to the booking and the room experience could lift them onto par or above. Extending the live nights or tightening the consistency of the sets gives guests more chances to encounter the program that earns the resort's best entertainment reviews.

Second, address the common-areas audio directly. The watch-item is not the live rooms, it is the ambient background music around the pools and in shared spaces, which a minority of guests find intrusive. Treating in-venue live programming and open-area background sound as two separate decisions, with the live rooms as the draw and the common-area audio dialed to fit a luxury setting, resolves the most repeated complaint without touching what guests like.

Both fit the before-and-after picture. Entertainment's share of conversation has settled lower in the Onstage era while the overall rating held steady, so the path forward is to make the live rooms a larger and more reliable part of why guests talk about The Phoenician, handled by one team across the venues.

Methodology

Coverage statement: This report analyzes 6,010 captured public guest reviews. Platform-level public totals were not refreshed for every source in the latest capture status, so this report does not present a summed public-review universe. The reviews analyzed span Oct 2002 to Jun 2026. The figures below are based on the captured review set, not an assertion that every public review on every platform was downloaded. Where a source's captured count is below its public total, the difference is typically rating-only entries (a star score with no written text), which carry no analyzable content and are not part of the figures.

This report draws on 6, 010 verified public guest reviews of The Phoenician Resort and Spa across Booking.com, TripAdvisor, Expedia, and Google, with the bulk in English. Theme shares, venue mentions, and the entertainment read are derived from that review set, and the before-and-after view is anchored to October 2017, when Onstage assumed the resort's entertainment program. That comparison is directional rather than controlled, since each period reflects its full guest experience and the mix of platforms active at the time. We also scanned open-web sources including forums, social posts, and Yelp to corroborate the venue-level signal.
