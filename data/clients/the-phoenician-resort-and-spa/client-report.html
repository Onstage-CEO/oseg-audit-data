The Phoenician's entertainment story lives in two specific rooms: the dueling pianos at The Phoenician Tavern on Saturday nights, and the live music at Thirsty Camel from Thursday through Saturday. These are the property's named entertainment anchors, and they are where guests are forming their impressions of your evening offer.

Entertainment is one meaningful theme in your review set, appearing in roughly 13 percent of reviews. It does not lead the conversation the way service, food and beverage, and the pools do, but it is a steady presence. The most useful finding for you is balance: in-venue entertainment rates about on par with the property's overall standard rather than pulling clearly above or below it. The work is not a blanket fix. It is finding which rooms and formats sit above the line and which sit just below.

The clearest watch-item is not about a performance at all. It concerns sound spilling into spaces meant to be quiet, the pools, the dining rooms, and even common areas, where a portion of guests want calm rather than a soundtrack. That tension between a lively evening program and quiet daytime enjoyment is the specific thing to manage here.

The satisfaction landscape

Service and staff lead this property's review set by a wide margin, followed closely by food and beverage and by the pools and amenities, which sit together as the second tier. Cleanliness and rooms, value, and check-in waits follow. Entertainment and atmosphere sit lower in the mix at roughly 13 percent of reviews, which places it as a supporting theme rather than a headline driver for these guests.

This matters because entertainment at The Phoenician is encountered mostly inside the food and beverage experience, not as a standalone draw. The dueling pianos sit inside a restaurant and bar, the live music sits inside a lounge. When a guest talks about the evening program here, they are usually talking about a meal or a drink that had music attached to it. That overlap with your strongest themes is an advantage, because the rooms carrying the entertainment are already rooms guests rate well.

Entertainment deep-dive

The headline entertainment story is the dueling pianos at The Phoenician Tavern on Saturday nights. The Tavern carries a solid guest rating, and the standout review pairs the dueling pianos with the casual menu, the boozy milkshakes, and what the guest called the best wine list of any bar or restaurant on the property. This is your signature entertainment, a format guests remember and name.

The second anchor is the live music at Thirsty Camel, running Thursday through Saturday. It rates slightly higher than the Tavern and draws a mellow, date-night ambiance, with guests describing staying through to the end of the set. Between these two rooms, the pattern is clear: the bars and lounges are where entertainment carries weight, with the most guest mentions by a wide margin, and the restaurants follow as the second venue group. The pool decks and common areas register far fewer entertainment mentions.

Against the property's own overall standard, in-venue entertainment rates about on par, neither a standout above the line nor a drag below it. The Tavern and Thirsty Camel are doing the lifting and doing it well. The specific watch-item is the inverse problem: amplified sound reaching places guests expect to be quiet. One critical account describes music carrying across all three pools, into the dining rooms, and into common spaces, leaving no calm seat. That is a placement and zoning issue, not a quality-of-act issue, and it sits apart from the live-music rooms that guests praise.

The guest voice

Most guests who mention entertainment here speak warmly about it, and the in-venue entertainment rating matches the property's overall standard. The praise clusters around live music at Thirsty Camel and the lobby lounge, and around the dueling pianos at the Tavern. At the same time, a meaningful minority were not satisfied: while most guests praise the entertainment, 34 rated their entertainment experience at one or two stars. The sharpest critical note is not about a weak act but about amplified sound reaching the pools, dining rooms, and common areas where some guests wanted quiet. Both readings are real, and the honest case for improvement rests on that sound-placement tension rather than on the quality of the performances.

The opportunity

The opening for Onstage at The Phoenician is to protect what works and resolve the one tension that pulls guests apart. The dueling pianos at the Tavern and the Thursday-through-Saturday live music at Thirsty Camel are proven draws; the goal is consistency across the week and across rooms, so the strength of a Saturday set is not a matter of luck for the guest who happens to be there that night.

The clearest action is sound zoning. The critical signal is specific: music designed to energize the evening is reaching pool seating, dining rooms, and common areas during hours when a portion of guests want calm. Defining where sound belongs, and keeping quiet spaces genuinely quiet while the bars and lounges carry the lively program, addresses the loudest complaint without touching the performances guests love.

There is also room to extend the evening program beyond the two anchor rooms in a measured way, given how few entertainment mentions the pool decks and common areas currently draw. Seasonal activations such as the property's Halloween celebration, and the family-facing daytime activity already in place, suggest an audience open to more, provided any addition respects the same quiet-versus-lively boundaries. One team handling booking, scheduling, and consistency across these rooms keeps that balance intentional rather than incidental.

Methodology

Coverage statement: This report analyzes 6,010 captured public guest reviews. Platform-level public totals were not refreshed for every source in the latest capture status, so this report does not present a summed public-review universe. The reviews analyzed span Oct 2002 to Jun 2026. The figures below are based on the captured review set, not an assertion that every public review on every platform was downloaded. Where a source's captured count is below its public total, the difference is typically rating-only entries (a star score with no written text), which carry no analyzable content and are not part of the figures.

This report draws on 6, 010 verified public guest reviews of The Phoenician Resort and Spa across Booking.com, TripAdvisor, Expedia, and Google, the large majority in English. Within that set, 417 reviews touched on entertainment and atmosphere, and findings are grounded in named venues, guest ratings, and the actual language of those reviews. Open-web signal from forums, editorial travel coverage, and social platforms was reviewed for context only; no figure, benchmark, or claim has been introduced beyond what the verified review base supports.
