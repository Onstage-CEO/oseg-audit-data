The clearest entertainment signal at The Phoenician Resort and Spa is not on a stage, it is at the pools. A recurring set of reviews describes the music piped across the grounds as too loud for guests who came to relax, with one TripAdvisor guest noting they could not hear a podcast over noise-canceling headphones by mid-morning. That single tension, ambient sound that some guests read as atmosphere and others read as intrusion, defines the entertainment conversation here more than any one act.

Entertainment is a smaller part of what guests talk about at this property, roughly one in ten reviews. Service and staff lead the conversation by a wide margin, with food and beverage and the pools and amenities close behind. That order matters: entertainment is not what makes or breaks a stay here, so it should be treated as a focused, high-leverage lever rather than the headline.

Where entertainment does come up, it rates about on par with the property's overall standard. The opportunity is not a turnaround, it is precision: protect the formats guests already praise, such as the dueling pianos and the lounge live music, and resolve the ambient-sound complaint that pulls the common areas below the line.

The satisfaction landscape

Service and staff dominate the review set for The Phoenician, appearing in well over half of all reviews, followed by food and beverage and then the pools and amenities. Cleanliness and rooms, value, and check-in wait times all draw more attention than entertainment, which sits last among the tracked themes at roughly a tenth of reviews. For this property's guests, the experience is anchored in attentive service and strong dining, and entertainment functions as a supporting layer on top of that. The practical implication is that entertainment wins here are additive. They do not need to carry the property, but a sour note in the common areas can undercut the relaxation that the pools and spa are meant to deliver, which is why the ambient-sound issue matters more than its raw share of mentions suggests.

Entertainment deep-dive

The signature entertainment story at this property is the dueling pianos at The Phoenician Tavern, running Friday and Saturday evenings. Guests who come specifically for it speak warmly, describing the pianists as talented and the evening as a genuinely good time, with one Yelp guest advising others to arrive early enough to order dinner before the kitchen closes. The Tavern's own rating sits modestly below the resort's overall mark, so the pianos read as a draw that brings people in while the surrounding food-and-service experience is where the venue has room to tighten.

Across the named venues, in-venue entertainment rates about on par with the property's overall standard, and the pattern is consistent. The bars and lounges draw the most entertainment mentions, and live music in the lounge, including at the Thirsty Camel, comes up repeatedly as a treat. The restaurants are the next most mentioned, with live music at the steakhouse cited as part of what made dinner memorable. These in-venue, evening formats are working.

The watch-item sits in the third group, the pool decks and common areas. Several guests describe music broadcast across the grounds, and even into bathrooms, at a volume that interrupts the quiet they expected, with one noting it began around 9:30 in the morning. This is not a complaint about the entertainment programming itself but about ambient sound design in spaces guests use to unwind. It is the one format pulling below the line, and it is the most actionable.

The guest voice

Most guests who mention entertainment at The Phoenician speak positively, and a large share of those rated their experience five stars. At the same time, a meaningful minority were unhappy: twelve guests rated their entertainment experience at one or two stars, and their complaints cluster tightly around volume and ambient music in the pools and common areas rather than the live programming. That number is small against the praise, but it is specific and consistent enough to act on. The quotes below are grouped into critical and praise so both sides are visible.

The opportunity

The most direct opportunity is to resolve the ambient-sound tension in the pool decks and common areas. The praise concentrates on programmed, in-venue entertainment in the evenings, while the criticism concentrates on background music in daytime relaxation spaces. Treating those as two different decisions, protecting the live formats and rezoning or softening the daytime poolside sound so guests have quieter areas to sit, addresses the specific complaint without touching what guests already value.

On the programming side, the dueling pianos at The Phoenician Tavern and the live music in the lounges and at the steakhouse are proven draws. The lever here is consistency and reach: making the weekend pianos easy to discover ahead of arrival, and extending the kind of live-music moments guests already praise across the bars and restaurants on a dependable schedule. Onstage can support both as one team, aligning evening live entertainment with a daytime sound plan that lets the pools stay restful. None of this requires reinventing the program; it asks for sharper placement of formats this property's guests already respond to.
