The defining entertainment story at The Phoenician Resort and Spa is a split in how live music lands across two settings. At The Phoenician Tavern, the Friday and Saturday dueling pianos draw guests who come specifically for the show and leave happy, while at the pool decks and common areas the music itself is the most consistent complaint, with daytime guests describing it as too loud to relax. The same programming instinct, live and amplified sound, reads as a draw in one venue and an irritant in another.

Entertainment is a smaller, more focused part of the conversation here than at many resorts. It appears in roughly one in ten reviews, well behind service and staff, which lead by a wide margin, and food and beverage. This is not the property's defining theme, and we are not going to treat it as one. It is a targeted, high-leverage area where a few specific adjustments can lift sentiment without touching what is already working.

In-venue entertainment rates about on par with the property's overall standard. The opportunity is not a blanket fix but a sorting exercise: protect and extend the formats guests seek out by name, and resolve the one recurring watch-item at the pool that pulls the score down.

The satisfaction landscape

Across this review set, service and staff dominate the conversation, appearing in more than half of all reviews, followed by food and beverage and then the pool and amenities. These three themes are what guests come to The Phoenician to talk about. Cleanliness and rooms, value, and check-in wait times follow, and entertainment and atmosphere sits at the back of the pack at roughly one in ten reviews. For these specific guests, entertainment is an accent on a stay defined mostly by people, dining, and the grounds, not the headline reason they book. That placement matters because it tells you where to aim: entertainment work here should reinforce the dining and pool experiences guests already prize, rather than try to become a separate attraction.

Entertainment deep-dive

The signature entertainment story at this property is the dueling pianos at The Phoenician Tavern on Friday and Saturday nights. Guests come for the show specifically, praise the talent of the pianists, and pass along practical advice to one another, such as arriving early to order dinner before the kitchen closes. The Tavern itself carries a more mixed rating than the property overall, which suggests the pianos are a genuine draw operating inside a venue that has other things to sort out, food and timing among them. That is useful: the entertainment is pulling its weight, and there is room to make the surrounding experience match it.

Live music also surfaces warmly across the bars and lounges and the restaurants. Guests mention live music in the lounge as a treat, live music at the steak house during dinner, and the half-price happy hour with live music. The thirsty camel lounge draws specific praise for its live music. The pattern is clear: where live music is paired with dining or a seated lounge setting, it reads as an asset and reinforces the food and beverage strength that guests already rank highly.

The watch-item is concentrated at the pool decks and common areas. The recurring complaint there is not the absence of atmosphere but the presence of too much of it: pulsing, amplified music starting in the morning that guests describe as making it hard to read, relax, or hold a conversation, with one guest noting it carried into the bathrooms. In-venue entertainment overall rates about on par with the property's standard, so this is not a property-wide problem. It is a specific mismatch between programming energy and what daytime pool guests are looking for.

The guest voice

The honest read is two-sided. Most guests who mention entertainment praise it, and five-star entertainment mentions far outnumber the critical ones; the live music in the lounges and at dinner is consistently called a treat. At the same time, a meaningful minority were unhappy: twelve guests rated their entertainment experience at one or two stars, and their complaints cluster tightly around pool-area music being too loud and too early in the day. That small but specific count is the real case for a targeted adjustment, and it is more credible than overstating a problem that most guests did not have.

The opportunity

Three concrete moves fit what this property's reviews actually show. First, protect and build on the dueling pianos at The Phoenician Tavern. It is the entertainment guests name and seek out, and it is the clearest anchor to extend, whether by reinforcing the surrounding service and dining flow on show nights or by considering whether a comparable signature format could work in another lounge. Second, resolve the pool-area sound. The complaints are specific and repeatable: volume, start time, and reach into seating and bathrooms. Defining quieter zones, a later start, or a calmer daytime soundscape addresses the single most common entertainment grievance without removing atmosphere from the evening venues where guests welcome it. Third, lean into the seated live-music formats that already work, in the bars and lounges, at the restaurants, and at happy hour, since these reinforce the food and beverage strength guests rank near the top.

Onstage can act as one partner across these venues, matching the right format to each setting so the energy at the pool, the lounges, and the Tavern each fits the guest who is actually there. The aim is consistency, not more volume.
