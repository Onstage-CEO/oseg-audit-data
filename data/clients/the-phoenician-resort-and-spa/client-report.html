The defining entertainment story at The Phoenician Resort and Spa lives in two named rooms: the dueling pianos on Saturday nights at The Phoenician Tavern, and the live music Thursday through Saturday at Thirsty Camel. These are the moments guests single out by name, and they rate well above the noise of a typical resort bar review. The Tavern and Thirsty Camel both sit just below the property's overall standard in their own venue ratings, which tells you the in-venue music is doing its job while the dining and service mechanics around it carry the occasional dip.

Entertainment is one meaningful theme here, not the headline. It appears in roughly 13 percent of reviews, well behind service and staff, food and beverage, and the pools and amenities, which dominate what guests write about. Where guests do mention atmosphere and live music, they rate it about on par with how they rate the resort as a whole. That is a healthy baseline, not a problem to fix.

The one watch-item is specific and worth naming: a meaningful minority of guests experience the property's ambient sound, the piped music across the pool decks, restaurants, and common areas, as intrusive rather than atmospheric. The live-music venues are an asset. The background sound design in the shared spaces is where the friction sits.

The satisfaction landscape

Guests at The Phoenician write first and foremost about service and staff, which appears in nearly two thirds of reviews, followed closely by food and beverage and by the pools and amenities, each in just under half. Cleanliness and room, value, and check-in and wait times follow. Entertainment and atmosphere sits at the bottom of this set at roughly 13 percent, which is exactly what you would expect for a resort whose reputation is built on service and grounds rather than on a nightlife identity. This matters because the entertainment guests do encounter is mostly woven into the food and beverage experience, the live music at the Tavern and Thirsty Camel, the band that turns a dinner into a memory. For these guests, entertainment is not a separate reason to book; it is a quality that lifts the venues they were already visiting.

Entertainment deep-dive

The signature entertainment story is the Saturday dueling pianos at The Phoenician Tavern. Guests who catch it describe it as genuinely fun, and they pair it with the Tavern's casual menu, its boozy milkshakes, and what one guest called the best wine list of any bar or restaurant on the property. The Tavern carries a solid venue rating across its reviews, sitting modestly below the resort's overall mark, which suggests the music is a draw while the broader dining experience occasionally varies.

Thirsty Camel is the second pillar, with live music Thursday through Saturday. The pattern in its reviews is consistent: guests come for drinks, stay until the music ends, and credit the mellow ambiance and attentive staff. Like the Tavern, its venue rating sits just under the property line, which places both live-music rooms about on par with this property's overall standard rather than above or below it. The lobby lounge also draws repeated praise for live music in the evening alongside its drink and appetite offering.

So the in-venue, performance-led entertainment is working. The specific watch-item is different in kind. A share of guests describe the ambient, piped music across the three pools, the restaurants, and the common areas as too loud and inescapable, to the point that it shaped their stay negatively. That is a sound-design and zoning issue in the shared spaces, separate from the live acts, and it is the clearest pattern pulling below the line.

The guest voice

Most guests who mention entertainment praise it, and the live-music nights at Thirsty Camel and the Tavern generate the warmest write-ups in the set. At the same time, an honest read names both sides: while the majority are positive, 34 guests rated their entertainment-related experience one or two stars. That real number, not an inflated reading of the negative, is the case for attention. The critical voices cluster around ambient sound in the shared spaces rather than the live performances themselves.

The opportunity

The most actionable finding is the gap between the live music guests love and the background sound that a minority find intrusive. The Tavern's Saturday pianos and Thirsty Camel's Thursday-through-Saturday band are proven formats here; the opportunity is to extend that quality of programmed, intentional entertainment into more evenings and into the lobby lounge, which already draws praise for its evening music, while tightening the volume and zoning of the piped audio across the pool decks and restaurants so guests who want a quiet table can find one.

Because entertainment at The Phoenician rides inside the food and beverage experience rather than standing alone, the highest-return moves are venue-anchored: a consistent, well-matched live program in the rooms guests already favor, scheduled across more of the week and running well into the evening, with the shared-space sound designed so it supports the room rather than overwhelms it. One partner handling both the live talent and the ambient-sound design keeps that experience coherent from the pool deck through dinner.

Methodology

Platform-level public totals were not refreshed for every source in the latest capture status, so this report does not present a summed public-review universe. The reviews analyzed span Oct 2002 to Jun 2026. The figures below are based on the captured review set, not an assertion that every public review on every platform was downloaded. Where a source's captured count is below its public total, the difference is typically rating-only entries (a star score with no written text), which carry no analyzable content and are not part of the figures.

This report draws on 6, 010 verified public guest reviews of The Phoenician Resort and Spa across Booking.com, TripAdvisor, Expedia, and Google, with entertainment and atmosphere signal isolated from roughly 417 relevant mentions and read at the named-venue level. Venue ratings, theme shares, and the count of low-star entertainment mentions are taken directly from that review set. Open-web and forum scanning provided supporting context only; no claim here rests on an unverified source.
