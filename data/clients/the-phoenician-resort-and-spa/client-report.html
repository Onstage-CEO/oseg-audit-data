The most telling fact about The Phoenician Resort and Spa is what guests choose to talk about. Across more than four thousand reviews on Google, TripAdvisor, Expedia, and Booking.com, the conversation is dominated by service and staff and by food and beverage. Entertainment and atmosphere is a smaller, more specific part of the picture, named in roughly one in eleven reviews. This is not a property where guests arrive primarily for a nightly show. It is a property where the in-venue experience lives inside the dining rooms, the bars and lounges, and the pool and common areas.

Where guests do speak to entertainment and atmosphere, they rate it roughly in line with the property's already high overall standard. In other words, the atmosphere is holding the same bar the rest of the resort holds, neither dragging it down nor pulling notably ahead. That puts entertainment in a useful position: a focused lever rather than a problem to fix.

The signal worth acting on sits inside the restaurants and bars. These are the two most-mentioned in-venue settings here, and they are where the resort's strongest praise and its sharpest service complaints both land. The atmosphere of those rooms is tied directly to how the service in them is felt, which is exactly the theme guests care about most.

This report frames entertainment as a high-leverage opportunity within the dining and lounge experience, not as the property's defining story.

The satisfaction landscape

The theme mix here is clear about what these guests value. Service and staff leads by a wide margin and appears in the majority of reviews. Food and beverage is the second most-discussed theme, named in a large share of reviews. The pool and amenities follow. Value and price, cleanliness and room, and check-in and wait each draw a smaller but real volume of comment. Entertainment and atmosphere sits at the lower end of the named themes.

For this audience, that ordering matters. The Phoenician's guests are not measuring their stay against a show schedule. They are measuring it against how they were treated and what they ate and drank. Entertainment and atmosphere is the connective layer underneath those two leading themes: it is the feel of the restaurants and bars where service and food are delivered. That is why a smaller share of mentions still represents a meaningful lever. Improving the atmosphere of the dining and lounge venues works directly on the two themes guests talk about most.

Entertainment deep-dive

In-venue entertainment and atmosphere at The Phoenician rates about on par with the resort's overall standard, which is itself high. The right read is not a single blanket verdict but a venue-by-venue one. Three settings carry this conversation: the restaurants, which draw the most mentions, the bars and lounges close behind, and the pool decks and common areas.

The restaurants are the center of gravity. They generate the largest volume of in-venue comment, and they are also where the property's strongest and weakest guest experiences are described. The same dining setting that produces warm praise for friendly, attentive in-room and on-floor service also produces the resort's most pointed complaints about slow service and inconsistent attention at dinner. The atmosphere of these rooms is inseparable from the service felt inside them. When the floor runs well, the room feels like the rest of the property; when it lags, the atmosphere is what the guest remembers as having fallen short.

The bars and lounges are the second pillar and the clearest opportunity for a deliberate atmosphere program. They draw nearly as many mentions as the restaurants. On the open web, prospective Scottsdale visitors discussing nightlife and evening energy tend to point toward the off-property club and bar district rather than to the resort itself, which suggests the on-site lounge experience is read as calm rather than as a draw in its own right. That is a position to build from, not a flaw.

The pool decks and common areas round out the picture with a steady volume of mentions, consistent with a daytime resort atmosphere rather than an evening entertainment one.

Because the entire program sits in the period after the reopening, there is no pre-renovation baseline in the review record to compare against. Everything here reflects the current, post-reopening property, which means the venue patterns above describe the resort as guests experience it today.

The opportunity

The clearest opening for Onstage is the bars and lounges. They are the second most-mentioned in-venue setting at The Phoenician, yet the evening energy guests associate with Scottsdale is currently described as living off-property. A consistent, well-matched live atmosphere in the lounges, set to the resort's calm luxury register rather than a club register, gives the property an evening draw of its own without changing its character. This is the kind of program that can shift the on-site lounge from a quiet room into a reason guests stay in for the evening.

The second opening is the restaurants. The atmosphere of these rooms rises and falls with the consistency of service inside them, which is the theme guests care about most. A defined musical and atmospheric standard for the dining venues, applied the same way every night, supports the floor team during the exact slow-service moments that produce the property's sharpest reviews. The goal is to make the room feel intentional even on a busy night.

Both moves are handled by one team, one program brief, and one standard across the venues, so the resort's high overall bar is held consistently from the dining rooms to the lounges. Because entertainment here is a focused part of the conversation rather than the whole, the work should stay precise: the specific venues above, the specific evening hours, and a feel that matches what these guests already praise.

Methodology

Coverage statement: This report analyzes 4,203 captured public guest reviews. Platform-level public totals were not refreshed for every source in the latest capture status, so this report does not present a summed public-review universe. The reviews analyzed span Oct 2017 to Jun 2026. The figures below are based on the captured review set, not an assertion that every public review on every platform was downloaded.

This audit is built only on verified public guest reviews for The Phoenician Resort and Spa, drawn from Google, TripAdvisor, Expedia, and Booking.com, alongside a scan of open-web sources including forums, social platforms, and Yelp. The review base is large and overwhelmingly in English, with deep coverage on Google and TripAdvisor in particular. Findings reflect what guests actually wrote, with entertainment and atmosphere isolated to its named in-venue settings rather than inferred from general sentiment.
