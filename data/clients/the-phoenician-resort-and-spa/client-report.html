At The Phoenician Resort and Spa, the defining entertainment story is the dueling pianos at The Phoenician Tavern on Friday and Saturday nights. Guests who come specifically for that show speak well of it, and it gives the property a named, repeatable evening draw that most resorts in Scottsdale cannot point to. That is the headline. The watch-item sits at the other end of the day: the music played across the pool decks and common areas during daytime hours, which a meaningful minority of guests describe as too loud to relax against.

Entertainment is a focused part of the conversation here, not the center of it. Roughly one in ten reviews touch on it, while service and staff and food and beverage are what these guests talk about most. So the right frame is not whether entertainment defines the resort, it is which specific venues and formats are pulling above the line and which one is pulling below it.

On the aggregate, in-venue entertainment rates about on par with the property's overall standard. The live music programs read as a quiet strength; the daytime pool sound level is the single specific item worth managing.

The satisfaction landscape

The conversation at The Phoenician is led by service and staff, which appears in well over half of reviews, followed by food and beverage and then the pool and amenities. Cleanliness and room, value, and check-in wait times follow. Entertainment and atmosphere sits last among the named themes, at about a tenth of reviews.

That ordering matters for how you act on entertainment here. These guests are choosing the resort for its people, its dining, and its pools, and entertainment reaches them mostly inside those same settings, the live music in the lounges, the bands in the steakhouse, the sound design around the pools. It is not a standalone draw competing for attention; it is the texture layered onto experiences guests already rate highly. That makes it high-leverage to get right and equally high-leverage to get wrong, because the same pool deck that anchors the amenities story is where the loudest complaints originate.

Entertainment deep-dive

The signature entertainment at the property is the dueling pianos at The Phoenician Tavern, programmed Friday and Saturday nights. Guests who come for it describe the pianists as fun and talented and treat the show as a destination in its own right, with practical advice to other guests about arriving early enough to order dinner before the kitchen closes. The Tavern itself carries a rating somewhat below the property's overall standard, which is the useful nuance: the entertainment is a reason to come, and the surrounding food and timing experience is where that venue has room to tighten.

The other clear bright spot is Thirsty Camel, where guests describe live music most nights and an ambiance they call uplifting and easy to settle into. Between the Tavern and Thirsty Camel, the pattern is consistent: the bars and lounges, which draw the most entertainment mentions of any setting here, are where live music lands best, and they are reinforced by live music in the restaurants, including the steakhouse, where guests pair it with the food and service they already praise.

Against this property's own standard, in-venue entertainment rates about on par overall, with the lounges and restaurants pulling slightly above the line and one format pulling below it. That format is the recorded music across the pool decks and common areas. The specific complaint is not about live performance at all; it is daytime sound level, music described as starting mid-morning and carrying into seating areas and even restrooms, loud enough that guests seeking a quiet poolside read could not get one. That is a sound-design and scheduling issue inside the very amenity, the pools, that ranks among the property's top three themes.

The guest voice

Most guests who mention entertainment speak warmly of it, and the live music in the lounges and restaurants is a recurring source of praise. At the same time, an honest read names both sides: of the guests who commented on their entertainment and atmosphere experience, 13 rated it one or two stars. Those lower ratings cluster around a single, specific issue, the volume and reach of the pool and common-area music during the day, rather than the live performances themselves. That distinction is the most actionable thing in the guest voice here.

The opportunity

There are two concrete moves for The Phoenician. First, protect and extend what already works. The dueling pianos at The Phoenician Tavern and the live music at Thirsty Camel are named, guest-recognized draws; a more consistent and well-staffed live program across the lounges and the steakhouse builds directly on the bars-and-lounges strength that already leads the property's entertainment mentions, and pairs naturally with the service and dining that these guests rate most highly. Second, resolve the one specific watch-item: the daytime sound level across the pool decks and common areas. That means zoned audio with genuine quiet seating, a later or softer start to amplified music, and sound that does not carry into restrooms and reading areas. Fixing it converts the property's most repeated entertainment complaint without touching the live programming that guests value.

Onstage can support both as one partner: steadying and scheduling the live music across the Tavern, Thirsty Camel, and the dining rooms, and advising on the pool and common-area sound design so the atmosphere reads as inviting rather than intrusive.
