At The Phoenician, the conversation guests are having is overwhelmingly about people and plates. Service and staff lead by a wide margin, with food and beverage close behind, and that is the bar against which everything else at this property gets measured. In-venue entertainment and atmosphere sits in a different place. It surfaces in roughly one in eleven reviews, and where it does come up, it rates below the standard the rest of the property sets. That is the headline for this report: a resort that performs strongly almost everywhere, with entertainment as the one area trailing its own high bar.

This is not a property with an entertainment problem so much as an entertainment gap. The strength of the brand and the service makes the gap more visible, not less. When a guest arrives expecting the same polish across every touchpoint, the quieter evening atmosphere stands out by contrast.

Read this report as a focused opportunity rather than a broad fix. The leverage is narrow and specific, concentrated in a few named venues, and that is exactly why it is worth acting on.

The satisfaction landscape

The theme mix here tells a clear story. Service and staff dominate the guest conversation, mentioned in well over half of all reviews, followed by food and beverage in roughly four in ten. Pool and amenities form a strong third pillar, and value, cleanliness, and check-in fill out the middle of the picture. Entertainment and atmosphere comes last among the tracked themes, present in under one in ten reviews.

That placement matters for how you read it. These are guests who notice and reward service and dining above all else, and they bring those same high expectations into the evening hours. Entertainment is not what brings them to The Phoenician, but it is part of the experience they are quietly measuring against the rest of the stay. A small share of the conversation, in a property this strong, is precisely where a focused improvement carries weight.

Entertainment deep-dive

When entertainment does come up at The Phoenician, the venues doing the talking are clear. Bars and lounges carry the largest share of mentions by a wide margin, with restaurants the clear second. The nightclub and live-music side and the pool decks and common areas register only lightly. So the evening atmosphere conversation at this resort is really a bar, lounge, and dining-room conversation. That is where guests form their impression of the property after dark, and that is where the rating sits below the resort's overall standard.

The pattern is consistent with what shows up in open-web discussion as well, where guests place the Scottsdale nightlife energy in the surrounding block of bars and clubs rather than inside the resort itself. The property's own family programming, the kids' clubs, clinics, and daytime activities, is well established. The adult evening atmosphere across the bars, lounges, and restaurants is the thinner part of the picture, and it is exactly the part that trails the bar set by service and food.

What is working is the foundation. The venues exist, the service and food around them are strong, and guests are already in these spaces in the evening. What needs watching is the atmosphere layer on top, the sense that the bars and lounges feel like a reason to stay in for the night rather than a place to pass through. Because every other element around these rooms performs well, lifting the atmosphere is an adjustment, not a rebuild.

The opportunity

The opportunity at The Phoenician is concentrated and practical. Start where the mentions are: the bars and lounges, then the restaurants. These rooms already carry strong service and food scores and already hold guests in the evening, so a consistent, well-fitted live atmosphere in those specific spaces is the highest-leverage move available. The aim is for the bars and lounges to feel like a destination for the evening rather than a stop on the way out to Scottsdale's nightlife block.

The nightclub and live-music venue and the pool decks register only lightly today, which makes them a second, optional layer rather than the priority. Daytime and family programming is already a documented strength, so the focus belongs squarely on the adult evening experience where the resort trails its own standard. A single team handling the planning and consistency across these venues keeps the atmosphere aligned with the polish guests already praise everywhere else. That alignment, matching the evening to the service and dining bar, is the whole of the opportunity here.

Methodology

The reading reflects how guests themselves describe the property in their own words, sorted by theme and by venue, and weighted toward English-language reviews, which make up the large majority.
