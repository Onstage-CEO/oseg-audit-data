The defining entertainment story at The Phoenician Resort and Spa is the dueling pianos at The Phoenician Tavern on Friday and Saturday nights, the one in-venue format guests single out by name and clearly enjoy. That is where the property's evening program earns its reputation, and it gives you a known, repeatable draw rather than a vague "lively atmosphere."

Entertainment is a focused part of the conversation here, not the headline. Service and staff lead by a wide margin, followed by food and beverage and the pools and amenities. Entertainment sits at roughly one in ten reviews. That makes it a smaller, high-leverage area: most guests are here for the service and the dining, and the evening program is something you can sharpen without it being the thing that defines the stay.

In-venue entertainment rates about on par with the property's overall standard, neither dragging it down nor pulling clearly ahead. The work is to find the specific venues and formats that sit above the line and lean into them, and to address the one recurring complaint that sits below it.

The satisfaction landscape

Reading the actual theme mix, service and staff dominate what guests choose to write about, appearing in more than half of all reviews. Food and beverage and the pools and amenities follow closely, each in roughly four of ten reviews. Cleanliness and rooms, value, and check-in waits trail behind. Entertainment and atmosphere come in last among the tracked themes, at about one in ten reviews.

For these specific guests, that order matters. The Phoenician is experienced first as a service and dining destination, and the entertainment lives inside that frame rather than competing with it. Guests encounter live music and the piano nights while they are at dinner or in a lounge, not as a separate ticketed event. So the most useful lens is not "how big is entertainment here" but "where, inside the dining and lounge experience guests already value, does the music help or hurt." That is a narrow and actionable question.

Entertainment deep-dive

The headline is the dueling pianos at The Phoenician Tavern, the property's signature in-venue entertainment, running Friday and Saturday. Guests who catch it describe it as fun and pair it naturally with the casual menu, the boozy milkshakes, and what one called the best wine list among the property's bars and restaurants. The Tavern carries a solid rating across its reviews, and the piano nights are clearly part of why people remember it.

The second venue that carries the program is Thirsty Camel, the lounge with live music Thursday through Saturday. Guests describe a mellow ambiance, attentive staff, and evenings where they came in for a drink and stayed until the music ended. Between these two, the pattern is consistent: the bars and lounges are the strongest entertainment surface here, drawing the most mentions and the warmest language. Restaurants come next, with guests recalling live music alongside dinner at the steak house. The pools and common areas draw a comparable number of mentions but a more mixed reaction.

In-venue entertainment rates about on par with the property's overall standard, and the venues themselves explain the spread. The Tavern and Thirsty Camel sit at the top end of the experience and are working well. The watch-item sits at the pools and common areas: at least one guest described music piped through speakers across the grounds, including in the restrooms, as loud and inescapable, with nowhere quiet to sit. That is a placement and volume issue in the ambient areas, distinct from the live programming in the lounges, and it is the specific thing pulling below the line.

The guest voice

Most guests who mention entertainment speak warmly about it, and the five-star mentions far outnumber the critical ones. At the same time, a meaningful minority were not satisfied: of the guests who commented on their entertainment experience, 13 rated it one or two stars. The praise clusters around the live music in the lounges and the piano nights; the criticism clusters around ambient music volume in the common areas rather than the live acts themselves. Both signals are real, and the contrast between them points directly at where the fix lives.

The opportunity

The most concrete move is to extend what already works at The Phoenician Tavern and Thirsty Camel. The dueling pianos run only Friday and Saturday and the Thirsty Camel music Thursday through Saturday, which leaves early-week evenings without a named draw. A reliable, lower-key live format on those quieter nights would give guests a reason to stay on property for dinner and drinks, building on the lounges that already earn the warmest comments.

The second move addresses the watch-item directly: the ambient music across the pools and common areas. Reviewing the volume, the speaker placement, and the restroom audio in those shared spaces would resolve the one recurring complaint without touching the live programming guests value. The goal is a clear line between the lounges, where music is the draw, and the common areas, where some guests want a quiet place to sit.

Across both, the value of one partner managing the live program is consistency: the piano nights and lounge music guests already remember, delivered on a predictable schedule, with the ambient sound in the shared areas handled so it supports the property's service-first reputation rather than working against it.
