The defining entertainment fact at The Phoenician is that the property's clearest signature event, dueling pianos at The Phoenician Tavern on Friday and Saturday nights, is also the venue carrying the property's lowest visible rating in this review set. The piano nights themselves draw warm, specific praise from guests who plan their evening around them. The Tavern as a whole sits below the resort's overall standard, which tells you the entertainment is working while something else in that room is not keeping pace.

Entertainment is a smaller part of the conversation here than service and food. About one in ten reviews touch atmosphere or entertainment at all, and service and food and beverage are what these guests talk about first. That is not a weakness to fix so much as a focused, high-leverage area: when guests do mention in-venue entertainment, they rate it about on par with how they rate the resort overall.

The most actionable tension in the data is not about live music being weak. It is about ambient sound. A meaningful group of low-star reviews describe music at the pools and common areas as too loud and too constant, including in places guests came to for quiet. So the same property earns five-star notes for live music in its lounges while losing stars over piped-in music by the pools. Those are two different problems with two different fixes.

The satisfaction landscape

For these guests, service and staff lead the conversation by a wide margin, followed by food and beverage and then the pools and amenities. Cleanliness and rooms, value, and check-in waits fill out the middle. Entertainment and atmosphere sit last among the named themes at roughly a tenth of reviews. That ordering matters because it tells you what frame guests arrive with: The Phoenician is read first as a service and dining resort, and entertainment is experienced as part of those settings rather than as a standalone draw. The practical implication is that entertainment wins or loses inside the bars and lounges and at the dinner table, which is exactly where the data places it.

Entertainment deep-dive

The headline is the dueling pianos at The Phoenician Tavern on Friday and Saturday nights. Guests who come specifically for the pianists describe them as fun and talented and a genuinely good time, with one reviewer recommending arriving early enough to order dinner before the kitchen closes. That detail is worth noting: the entertainment is the reason for the visit, and the food and beverage operation around it is the part guests have to work around. The Tavern overall rates below the resort's general standard, so the room is a strength on its performance nights with an operational watch-item attached to it.

Across the review set, in-venue entertainment rates about on par with the property overall. The pattern by venue is consistent. The bars and lounges draw the most entertainment mentions and the warmest ones, with live music in the lounge and at the Thirsty Camel lounge called out by name as a treat and a reason to return. Live music inside the restaurants, including the steak house, lands the same way, woven into a good dinner. So the live, in-venue formats are pulling at or above the line.

What pulls below the line is the pool decks and common areas, and the issue there is ambient music rather than live performance. Several lower-rated reviews describe pulse-pounding pool music starting early in the morning and piped sound in seating areas and even bathrooms that left guests with nowhere quiet to sit. This is the specific watch-item: the live entertainment program is well received, while the always-on background music in the common areas is actively costing the property stars from guests who came to relax.

The guest voice

Most guests who mention entertainment speak well of it, and the live music in the lounges and restaurants is a recurring bright spot. At the same time, a meaningful minority were not happy: of the guests who rated their entertainment or atmosphere experience, twelve gave it one or two stars. Those low-star reviews cluster around one theme, ambient music being too loud and too constant in the pools and common areas, rather than around the live performances themselves. The critical and praise quotes shown separately reflect that split, with the praise centered on live music and the criticism centered on piped-in sound.

The opportunity

Two concrete moves follow directly from this property's data. First, protect and extend what already works. The dueling pianos at The Phoenician Tavern and the live music in the lounges and restaurants are proven draws that guests name and return for. The opportunity is to make the performance nights more reliable and better supported operationally, including aligning kitchen and service timing with the show so the entertainment is not something guests have to work around. Where the Tavern rates below the resort standard, the fix is the room and the service around the music, not the music.

Second, treat the pool and common-area sound as a design problem, not a programming one. The low-star reviews are specific: volume, early start times, and the absence of any quiet seating. Zoning the sound so there are genuinely quiet areas, holding back the morning volume, and reconsidering music in bathrooms and fine dining spaces would directly address the complaints that are costing stars today. Onstage can work with one team across both the live program and the ambient sound design so the lounges keep their energy while the relaxation zones get their quiet back.

Methodology

Coverage statement: This report analyzes 3,420 captured public guest reviews. Across sources that returned a public total, 4,241 public reviews were identified. The reviews analyzed span Oct 2017 to Jun 2026. The figures below are based on the captured review set, not an assertion that every public review on every platform was downloaded.

This report draws on verified public guest reviews for The Phoenician Resort and Spa across Booking.com, TripAdvisor, Expedia, and Google, with additional signal from open-web sources including Yelp, travel forums, and social platforms. The entertainment read is built only from reviews that actually reference atmosphere, live music, or in-venue experience, and venue-level findings are tied to the named venues that appear in the review set. All ratings and counts are rendered directly from the underlying data rather than estimated. Because the dated reviews fall entirely after the property's reopening, the analysis reflects the current operation rather than a before-and-after comparison.
