The most distinctive entertainment finding at The Phoenician is that your signature live offerings sit inside two named venues, the dueling pianos at The Phoenician Tavern on Saturdays and live music at Thirsty Camel from Thursday through Saturday, and guests respond to both. The Tavern and Thirsty Camel each draw a strong volume of dedicated reviews, and the live-music nights are exactly what guests single out when they write about an evening they remember.

Entertainment is one meaningful theme among several here, appearing in roughly 13 percent of reviews. That places it behind service, food and beverage, and the pools, which is expected for a resort of this scale. What matters is that in-venue entertainment rates about on par with the property's overall standard rather than dragging below it. The signal is not a blanket problem to fix but a question of which formats and which spaces pull above or below your own line.

Two patterns define the situation. The live-music programming at Thirsty Camel and the Tavern pulls slightly below the property's overall average in those venues' own review scores, which says there is room to tighten consistency rather than rebuild. Separately, a smaller but real set of guests describe sound carrying into spaces meant to be quiet, including the pools and common areas. Both are reachable without touching what already works.

The satisfaction landscape

The Phoenician's review themes are led by service and staff, which dominates guest commentary, followed closely by food and beverage and by the pools and amenities, the two running near even. Cleanliness and rooms, value, and check-in wait times follow. Entertainment and atmosphere sits at the back of that ranking at about 13 percent of reviews.

That position is the honest context for this engagement. Guests come to The Phoenician for service, dining, and the pool experience first, and they talk about entertainment as part of an evening out at a bar or restaurant rather than as a primary reason for the stay. This matters because your entertainment lives almost entirely inside food and beverage venues, the two leading themes after service. When live music lands well at Thirsty Camel or the Tavern, it reinforces the dining experience guests already rate highly. When sound spills into the pool and common areas, it touches the amenity theme that ranks just as high. Entertainment here is not a standalone draw, it is a lever on the themes guests care most about.

Entertainment deep-dive

The headline entertainment story is the dueling pianos at The Phoenician Tavern on Saturday nights. The Tavern carries a large body of guest reviews and a solid average, and guests who catch the pianos describe them as a genuine reason the evening worked, alongside the casual menu, the boozy milkshakes the venue is known for, and what one guest called the best wine list on the property. This is your most identifiable in-venue entertainment format, and it should be treated as the headline.

Close behind is Thirsty Camel, which runs live music Thursday through Saturday. Guests describe a mellow, romantic ambiance and write about staying for drinks until the music ends. Reviews tied to live-music nights in this venue are among the most enthusiastic in the entire review set, with guests recalling a full band and dancing as a favorite memory of the stay.

Against the property's own standard, in-venue entertainment rates about on par. The two named venues, the Tavern and Thirsty Camel, both sit a touch below the resort's overall average in their venue-level scores, which points to consistency rather than a structural weakness. Bars and lounges carry the clear majority of entertainment mentions, restaurants account for most of the rest, and the pool and common areas a small share. The pattern is clear: entertainment at The Phoenician is a bar-and-lounge story anchored by two formats that already have guest goodwill. The specific watch-item is not the live music itself but sound discipline outside those venues, where a real, if smaller, set of guests describe music carrying into the pools, restaurants, and even quiet spaces where they did not want it.

The guest voice

The guest voice here is genuinely two-sided and should be read that way. Most guests who mention entertainment praise it, and the live-music nights at Thirsty Camel and the dueling pianos at the Tavern produce some of the warmest reviews in the set, with five-star entertainment mentions far outnumbering critical ones. At the same time, 34 guests rated their entertainment experience at the low end, and their concern is specific and consistent: sound carrying into spaces meant to be calm, including the pools and common areas. That real number, not an inflated reading of it, is the honest case for tightening sound placement while protecting what guests already love.

The opportunity

The opportunity at The Phoenician is to protect two formats that already work and resolve the one issue that pulls against them. The dueling pianos at the Tavern and live music at Thirsty Camel have guest goodwill; the practical work is consistency, making sure the quality guests rave about on the best nights is what every Thursday-through-Saturday guest experiences. A reliable booking standard across those nights would lift the venue-level scores that currently sit just under the property average.

The clearer fix is sound zoning. The critical reviews are not about the music being bad, they are about hearing it where guests expected quiet, around the pools, in the restaurants, and in common areas. Defining where amplified sound belongs and where it does not, and keeping the pool and common areas as lower-volume zones, directly addresses the watch-item without reducing the live programming guests come for. Because entertainment here lives inside your two leading themes after service, food and beverage and the pool experience, getting this right reinforces the themes guests already rate highest. Onstage can work as one team to standardize the live-music nights at both venues and align sound placement with the experience each space is meant to deliver.

Methodology

Coverage statement: This report analyzes 6,010 captured public guest reviews. Platform-level public totals were not refreshed for every source in the latest capture status, so this report does not present a summed public-review universe. The reviews analyzed span Oct 2002 to Jun 2026. The figures below are based on the captured review set, not an assertion that every public review on every platform was downloaded. Where a source's captured count is below its public total, the difference is typically rating-only entries (a star score with no written text), which carry no analyzable content and are not part of the figures.

This report draws on a verified body of public guest reviews for The Phoenician Resort and Spa across Booking.com, TripAdvisor, Expedia, and Google, with TripAdvisor and Google supplying the large majority. Entertainment findings are based on the reviews that actually mention in-venue entertainment and the venue-level review sets for The Phoenician Tavern and Thirsty Camel, read against the property's overall rating so that entertainment is judged by this property's own standard. Open-web scanning of forums, social platforms, and travel editorial provided supporting context only. We have stated the both-sided picture plainly, including the count of low-rated entertainment mentions, rather than overstating either side.
