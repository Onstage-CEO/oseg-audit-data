The Phoenician's entertainment story lives almost entirely indoors, at the bar. Of the roughly 417 reviews that touch on entertainment and atmosphere, the clear majority point to the property's bars and lounges, and the single most repeated detail is the Saturday dueling pianos at The Phoenician Tavern. That format, plus the Thursday-through-Saturday live music at Thirsty Camel, is what guests are actually describing when they talk about evening atmosphere here.

Entertainment is one meaningful theme among several, sitting at about 13 percent of reviews, well behind service and staff, food and beverage, and the pools and amenities, which dominate what guests write about. It is not the headline reason people choose this resort, but it is a recurring part of how they remember an evening.

On the numbers, in-venue entertainment rates about on par with the property's overall standard, not above it and not below it as a blanket verdict. The more useful read is by venue and format: the named bar programs draw warm, specific praise, while a smaller but real set of guests react against ambient music in the wrong places, particularly around the pools and inside the restaurants. That split is where the attention belongs.

The satisfaction landscape

Service and staff lead this property's review themes by a wide margin, appearing in roughly two thirds of all reviews, with food and beverage and the pools and amenities close behind each other in the mid-forties. Cleanliness and room, value and price, and check-in and wait follow. Entertainment and atmosphere sits last of the tracked themes at about 13 percent.

For these guests, that ordering matters. They come for service, dining, and the pool experience, and entertainment is something they encounter inside those settings rather than seek out on its own. That is why the strongest entertainment signal here is attached to dining and bar venues, and why the friction shows up where music overlaps with the pool and restaurant experiences that guests rate so highly. Entertainment at The Phoenician is judged as part of the atmosphere of those leading themes, not as a separate program.

Entertainment deep-dive

The signature entertainment here is the Saturday dueling pianos at The Phoenician Tavern. The Tavern carries a solid rating across its reviews, and the entertainment is a named part of why guests enjoy it: one guest described being lucky to catch dueling pianos on a Saturday night, alongside the casual menu, the boozy milkshakes the venue is known for, and what they felt was the best wine list on the property. That is a clear, repeatable draw tied to a specific night.

Close behind is Thirsty Camel, with live music Thursday through Saturday. Its rating sits just above the Tavern's, and guests describe a mellow ambiance where they came for drinks and stayed until the music ended. A separate review recalled a full band, soft rock rather than anything heavy, with parents and children up dancing. Together these two venues are the spine of the property's evening atmosphere, and both are bars and lounges, the category that accounts for the largest share of entertainment mentions by a wide margin.

Restaurants are the second entertainment setting, with the lobby lounge live music at night also drawing positive notes. Against this property's own overall standard, in-venue entertainment rates about on par, and the venue-level pattern explains why: the live-music bar programs pull slightly above the line, while the watch item pulls slightly below it. The specific watch item is ambient and piped music in places guests want quiet. One detailed account objected to speaker music across the pools, the fine-dining restaurants, and even the restrooms, with no quiet seating available. The pool decks and common areas draw only a small number of entertainment mentions, so the issue there is less about programming and more about volume and placement in spaces guests otherwise prize.

The guest voice

Most guests who mention entertainment speak well of it, and nearly 250 of the entertainment-related reviews are five-star, concentrated on the live-music bars. At the same time, a meaningful minority is not satisfied: 34 guests rated their entertainment-related experience one or two stars. That real number is the honest case for attention, and it points less at the live music itself than at where ambient music intrudes on quiet spaces. The opportunity

The clearest opening is to protect what already works and fix the one thing that does not. The live-music programs at Thirsty Camel and the dueling pianos at The Phoenician Tavern are named, dated, and repeatedly praised; they deserve consistent staffing and scheduling so a guest who comes for a Saturday night gets the experience the reviews promise. Onstage can help keep those formats reliably strong and varied across the Thursday-through-Saturday window without changing what guests already like about them.

The watch item is ambient music in the wrong places. Several guests value the pools and the fine-dining restaurants precisely because they are calm, and piped music there works against the property's leading themes of service, dining, and the pool experience. The practical move is to separate intentional live programming in the bars and lounges from background audio in quiet zones, with clear volume and placement standards around the pool decks and restaurant interiors. For seasonal moments such as the property's Halloween activation, Onstage can plan themed evening entertainment that lands in the bar and event spaces where guests expect energy, rather than spilling into the spaces they expect to be restful. One team handling booking, scheduling, and on-site coordination keeps that line clean.

Methodology

Coverage statement: This report analyzes 6,010 captured public guest reviews. Platform-level public totals were not refreshed for every source in the latest capture status, so this report does not present a summed public-review universe. The reviews analyzed span Oct 2002 to Jun 2026. The figures below are based on the captured review set, not an assertion that every public review on every platform was downloaded. Where a source's captured count is below its public total, the difference is typically rating-only entries (a star score with no written text), which carry no analyzable content and are not part of the figures.

This report draws on 6, 010 verified public guest reviews captured across Booking.com, TripAdvisor, Expedia, and Google, with TripAdvisor and Google providing the bulk of the set. Entertainment findings rest on the roughly 417 reviews that reference entertainment and atmosphere, with venue-level reads tied to the named bars and restaurants where guests describe live music. Open-web sources, including travel editorial and forum discussion, were scanned to confirm program context such as the seasonal Halloween activation and the property's family and activity offerings. We assert only what the reviews and verified sources support.
