The satisfaction landscape

The guest conversation at The Phoenician is led clearly by service and staff, which appears in nearly half of all reviews. Food and beverage comes next, followed closely by the pool and amenities. Cleanliness and rooms, value, and check-in wait times each draw a smaller share. Entertainment and atmosphere sits last among the named themes, in about 7 percent of reviews.

That ordering matters for how you read this report. These guests come to The Phoenician primarily for the people who serve them and the dining, and the atmosphere around those two things is where entertainment lives for them. Guests are not describing a separate nightlife product, they are describing the music and feel of the lounge, the lobby, and the restaurants they are already in. Entertainment here is the soundtrack to dining and the bar, not a standalone draw, so the highest-value moves sit inside those settings rather than alongside them.

Entertainment deep-dive

Across the entertainment mentions for this property, in-venue entertainment rates about on par with the resort's overall standard, with five-star mentions far outnumbering the low-star ones. The signal concentrates in three settings. Bars and lounges draw the most mentions, restaurants follow closely, and the pool decks and common areas account for a smaller share.

The clearest strength is in the bars and lounges. Guests name the lounge and the lobby happy hour directly, describing live music as a reason they enjoyed the space and, in one case, a reason they expect to return. Piano and lobby music in the afternoon and evening reads as a consistent, well-placed part of the experience. This is the format that pulls above the line, and it does so because it is built into where guests already gather.

The restaurants are more mixed, and the pattern is worth naming precisely. The on-site steakhouse earns praise as a dining room, and live entertainment in the restaurants is cited positively by repeat diners. The single specific problem is placement, not the music itself. A guest in a high-category suite positioned directly above the steakhouse reported late music and noise reaching the room. That is a containment and room-assignment issue tied to one location, not a verdict on restaurant entertainment overall. A separate review praised the music in the bar while objecting to food and drink pricing, which is a value comment rather than an entertainment one.

The pool decks and common areas register fewer mentions and no strong directional signal, so we would treat that as a smaller, secondary setting for now rather than a proven strength or weakness. Because every dated review here falls after the reopening, there is no clean before-and-after comparison to draw from; the read is of the property as it stands today.

The guest voice

Most guests who mention entertainment praise it, and the lounge and lobby music draw the warmest comments. At the same time, a meaningful minority were not satisfied: of the entertainment mentions, nine guests rated their experience one or two stars. That real count, set against a much larger group of five-star mentions, is the honest case for attention. It points to specific friction, most notably the noise carrying into a high-category suite above the steakhouse, rather than a broad problem with the program. The opportunity

Two concrete moves fit this property. First, protect and extend what already works in the bars and lounges. The lobby happy hour and the lounge music are named by guests as reasons they enjoyed their stay, so the priority is consistency: a reliable live-music schedule in those spaces, programmed for the afternoon-to-evening window guests describe, so the atmosphere they praise is present every time rather than by chance.

Second, resolve the steakhouse placement issue directly. The complaint is not about the music existing, it is about sound and late activity reaching the high-category suite above the restaurant. That can be managed through sound containment, a defined end time for amplified music, and a room-assignment flag so guests booking that suite are not placed directly over a late dining room without expectation set. Fixing one specific location addresses the clearest source of the low-star entertainment mentions.

Across both, the value is in one team handling the live-music programming and its placement so the lounge stays a strength and the restaurants stop generating noise complaints. Given that service and food lead the guest conversation here, entertainment's job is to support those settings, and that is exactly where these recommendations sit.
