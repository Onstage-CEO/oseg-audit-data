The resort-wide scores above are computed from the 299 property-level reviews in the current-state window (Jul 2025 to Jul 2026), a recency window of the 7,890 property-level review records captured deep across 2002, 2026, not the whole history. The entertainment findings draw on a separate, fidelity-checked layer of 6,708 all-time outlet-level OpenTable venue reviews; outlet ratings are venue-specific and are not blended into the resort-wide score.

The signature entertainment story at The Phoenician Resort and Spa is the dueling pianos on Saturday nights at The Phoenician Tavern. In the public review record, guests who catch that night describe it as a highlight of their stay, and the Tavern itself carries one of the stronger venue ratings on the property across its own large body of reviews. That is a real asset, and it is worth stating plainly before anything else, because it is what distinguishes this property's entertainment from a generic bar scene.

Entertainment is one meaningful theme among several here, appearing in roughly one in eight reviews. It sits below the themes that dominate the guest conversation at this property, service and staff, the pool and amenities, and food and beverage, all of which draw far more comment. So entertainment is not the headline of the guest experience at The Phoenician, but where it does surface, it is tied to a small number of named venues that guests remember.

A note on the evidence: only six current-window reviews carry verified in-scope entertainment signal. That is a directional read, not a property-wide measurement, and we have built this report primarily from the verified named-program and venue evidence rather than from that thin rating. Within that small set, most mentions lean positive, and one guest rated their entertainment experience at the low end. Both sides are real and we treat them as such.

The live program itself is on solid footing. The dueling pianos at The Phoenician Tavern, the live music at the Thirsty Camel lobby lounge, the solo piano at Afternoon Tea, and the resort-wide seasonal and holiday entertainment are all confirmed and currently running against booking records.

The satisfaction landscape

The guest conversation at The Phoenician is led decisively by service and staff, which appears in a clear majority of reviews, followed by the pool and amenities and then food and beverage. These three themes define what guests come to this property for and what they talk about afterward. Cleanliness and room, value and price, and check-in and wait timing follow.

Entertainment and atmosphere sits at the smaller end of that mix, in roughly one in eight reviews. For these specific guests, that placement matters in a particular way: this is a resort where dining and the pool decks carry the experience, and the live program lives inside those same spaces, the restaurants and the bars and lounges. So entertainment here is not a separate draw guests seek out on its own. It is the texture that a food-and-beverage-forward, pool-forward guest base encounters while doing the things they already came to do. That is why the venue-level signal, thin as it is, points almost entirely at the restaurants and the bars and lounges rather than at a standalone entertainment offering.

Entertainment deep-dive

The headline is the dueling pianos on Saturday nights at The Phoenician Tavern. Guests who happen to be on property that night single it out, and the Tavern carries a strong rating across its own large review base, one of the better-regarded venues on the property. This is confirmed and currently running as a Friday and Saturday residency. It is the clearest example of in-venue entertainment lifting a room rather than sitting in the background of it.

The second named venue is the Thirsty Camel lobby lounge, which runs live music on weekend evenings. Its own review base rates it well, and guests describe the mellow room and staying through the live set. That live music is confirmed and currently running, four nights weekly. Alongside it, Afternoon Tea at the Thirsty Camel lounge runs with live solo piano at every seating, also confirmed and currently running with a long booking history, a daytime and early-evening counterpart to the weekend-evening music.

Against this property's own overall standard, the picture is favorable but must be read carefully. The verified programming is genuinely landing at two venues guests already rate highly, the Tavern and the Thirsty Camel. That is what is working. The watch-item is specific and narrow: the entertainment signal in the review record is concentrated in the restaurants and the bars and lounges, with only a single mention touching the pool decks and common areas, even though those pool decks are one of the property's leading draws. Live poolside music here runs only as holiday special events, not as a regular series, so the pool experience that so many guests come for does not carry a recurring entertainment layer. That is the gap between where the entertainment is strong and where the guest base actually concentrates. We name only the venues that appear in the evidence, and the picture is directional given how few reviews carry entertainment signal.

The guest voice

The honest read is that guest voice on entertainment at The Phoenician is small and leans positive. Among the handful of current-window reviews that carry entertainment signal, most praise it, two at the top of the scale, and one guest rated their entertainment experience at the low end. That single low rating is not a property-wide verdict, and we will not present it as one. The praise clusters around the named live rooms, the dueling pianos at the Tavern and the live music at the Thirsty Camel. The quotes themselves are shown separately in two groups.

The opportunity

The single most valuable and reachable opportunity here is the pool. This property's guests come, in large numbers, for the pool and amenities, second only to service in what they talk about. Yet the entertainment that guests actually notice and reward lives indoors, at The Phoenician Tavern and the Thirsty Camel, while the pool decks and common areas surface in only one entertainment mention across the review set. Live poolside music at this property runs only around holidays as special events, not as a recurring part of the pool experience. So one of the resort's strongest reasons-to-book has essentially no recurring entertainment presence, while the confirmed, well-received live program sits in rooms guests visit later in the day.

That matters because it is a mismatch between where the entertainment strength is and where the guest energy is. The Tavern's dueling pianos and the Thirsty Camel's live music prove this property can put entertainment in a room and have guests remember it. The pool decks, which draw far more of the guest base, do not yet get that treatment on a recurring basis. This is a concrete, bounded gap, not a vague atmosphere problem, and it is fixable. It is also exactly the kind of question that rewards a proper on-site look rather than a guess from the review record.

We have named the gap deliberately without prescribing the answer. The validated, costed roadmap, what belongs where, in which spaces, and on what basis, is the work of the on-site diagnostic, where we walk the property, confirm the venue-by-venue picture against what the reviews only hint at, and hand you a plan built for this resort.

Methodology

Coverage statement: Property-level capture is deep and multi-year: 7,890 public review records of the resort were captured across Google, TripAdvisor, Expedia, and Booking spanning 2002, 2026. TripAdvisor and Booking were captured in full (captured count equals the platform's available total); Google and Expedia were captured to deep-crawl depth, which does not include every rating-only entry (a star with no written text) or reviews beyond the crawl limit, so this is a deep capture, not a claim that every public review was downloaded. The resort-wide scores in this report are computed from the 299 of those reviews that fall in the current-state window (Jul 2025 to Jul 2026); older reviews outside that window are retained but deliberately not blended into the current-state figures. Entertainment findings draw on a separate, fidelity-checked outlet-level layer of 6,708 all-time OpenTable venue reviews (J&G Steakhouse 3,436, Thirsty Camel 1,685, Mowry & Cotton 1,164, The Phoenician Tavern 423); those venue-specific ratings are not blended into the resort-wide hotel score. Property-level and OpenTable outlet-level together total 14,598 captured records (property-level 7,890 + OpenTable outlet-level 6,708).

This report is built on 299 verified public reviews for The Phoenician Resort and Spa drawn from Booking.com, TripAdvisor, Expedia, and Google across a one-year window, alongside the property's larger venue-level review bases and a scan of open-web and social sources. Programming status is confirmed against Onstage Entertainment Group booking records. Where the entertainment signal is thin, six current-window reviews carry in-scope entertainment mentions, we say so plainly and treat that rating as directional, leaning instead on the verified named-program and venue evidence. We name only venues and acts supported by that evidence, and we distinguish the live program from unrelated operational matters.
