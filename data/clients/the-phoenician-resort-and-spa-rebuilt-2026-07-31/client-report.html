The most distinctive fact about The Phoenician Resort and Spa is that your strongest entertainment story is already sitting inside one of your own venues, largely unassisted. On a Saturday night at The Phoenician Tavern, a guest described the dueling pianos as "lots of fun" and, in the same breath, called it the best wine list on the property. That is a signature entertainment moment tied to a specific room, and it is landing.

Entertainment is one meaningful theme among several here, present in roughly one in eight reviews. It sits well below the themes that define the guest conversation at this property, service and staff, the pool and amenities, and food and beverage. So this is not a report claiming entertainment is your biggest problem. It is a report showing that where entertainment does appear, the named venues carry it, and the signal, while small, is directionally positive.

A note on scale, stated plainly: only four current-window reviews contain verified in-scope entertainment signal. That is enough to point a direction, not enough to declare a property-wide pattern. We have built this read primarily from your confirmed named programs and your two entertainment venues, not from a handful of star ratings.

Across those four mentions the average sits below your two five-star entertainment reviews and is pulled by a single low-star mention, which is the honest shape of a small sample rather than evidence of a systemic gap.

The satisfaction landscape

Reading your actual theme mix, the guest conversation at The Phoenician is led by service and staff, which appears in a clear majority of reviews. The pool and amenities follow, then food and beverage, then cleanliness and room condition. Value, and check-in wait times, sit in the middle of the pack. Entertainment and atmosphere sits at the tail of that list, present in about one in eight reviews.

For these specific guests, that ordering matters. This is a property where people come for the pools, the dining, and the level of personal service, and they write about those things first. Entertainment is not what most guests arrive expecting to grade. That is precisely why the venues that do generate entertainment comment, The Phoenician Tavern and Thirsty Camel, are worth understanding closely: they are producing a positive signal inside a category most guests are not even primed to notice.

Entertainment deep-dive

The headline is the dueling pianos at The Phoenician Tavern, running Saturday evenings and confirmed as currently running. The Tavern holds a strong average across a large body of reviews, and the guest who caught the pianos on a Saturday night led with how much fun it was before praising the menu, the boozy milkshakes, and the wine list. That is the kind of moment guests remember and retell.

Your second entertainment room is Thirsty Camel, the lobby lounge, which runs live music on weekend evenings, confirmed and currently running. It rates even higher than the Tavern across its own reviews, and the guest voice there points to a mellow, attentive atmosphere where people arrived for a drink and stayed until the music ended. Thirsty Camel also hosts the Afternoon Tea, confirmed and currently running with live solo piano at the afternoon seatings, Thursday to Sunday. So this one lounge carries a daytime and an evening entertainment identity, both landing well.

Against the property's own high overall standard, the in-venue entertainment is doing its job. The two venues that generate entertainment comment both rate above the property average, and the praise is specific and tied to the act each room actually runs, pianos at the Tavern, live music at Thirsty Camel. The watch-item is not quality. It is visibility and scale. These positive moments are concentrated in two bars and lounges and confirmed for weekend evenings and afternoon seatings, which means the strongest entertainment you have is available on a narrow set of occasions, and only four current-window reviews surfaced entertainment at all. The pool decks and common areas generate a share of the entertainment mentions too, but the recurring, guest-verified live moments are the Tavern pianos and the Thirsty Camel music, not the pool.

The guest voice

The honest read of the guest voice is two-sided. Most guests who mention entertainment praise it, with two of the four in-scope mentions landing at five stars and naming a specific, memorable moment. At the same time, one guest rated their entertainment experience at the low end. On a base of four, that single low mention is not a property-wide verdict, but it is real and we are not going to explain it away. The opportunity

The single most valuable, reachable opportunity here is reach. Your best entertainment, the dueling pianos at The Phoenician Tavern and the live music at Thirsty Camel, is genuinely good and genuinely liked, but it is confined to a small set of weekend evenings and afternoon seatings inside two lounges, and it surfaces in only a handful of current reviews. On a property where guests write far more about the pools, the dining, and the service, the entertainment that could add a distinctive layer to a stay is currently something most guests never encounter and never mention. The positive signal is real but thin, and thin signal on strong material is a missed return, not a quality problem.

Why it matters to these guests: The Phoenician draws people who stay across multiple days and multiple areas of the resort, the pool decks, the restaurants, the common areas. Two well-liked lounge acts on limited occasions cannot touch most of that footprint or most of that stay. The upside is that this is fixable. The material is already working, the venues are already producing praise, and the gap is one of exposure rather than talent. What we are not going to do in a free document is hand over the plan for closing that gap. Naming the specific moves, and validating and costing them against your venues and your calendar, is the work of the on-site diagnostic, where the roadmap is delivered in full.

Methodology

Coverage statement: Property-level capture is deep and multi-year: 7,890 public review records of the resort were captured across Google, TripAdvisor, Expedia, and Booking spanning 2002, 2026. TripAdvisor and Booking were captured in full (captured count equals the platform's available total); Google and Expedia were captured to deep-crawl depth, which does not include every rating-only entry (a star with no written text) or reviews beyond the crawl limit, so this is a deep capture, not a claim that every public review was downloaded. The resort-wide scores in this report are computed from the 299 of those reviews that fall in the current-state window (Jul 2025 to Jul 2026); older reviews outside that window are retained but deliberately not blended into the current-state figures. Entertainment findings draw on a separate, fidelity-checked outlet-level layer of 6,708 all-time OpenTable venue reviews (J&G Steakhouse 3,436, Thirsty Camel 1,685, Mowry & Cotton 1,164, The Phoenician Tavern 423); those venue-specific ratings are not blended into the resort-wide hotel score. Property-level and OpenTable outlet-level together total 14,598 captured records (property-level 7,890 + OpenTable outlet-level 6,708).

This report is built on verified public reviews for The Phoenician Resort and Spa, drawn from Booking.com, TripAdvisor, Expedia, and Google across a defined recent analysis window, together with venue-level review evidence for the named outlets and a scan of open-web sources including travel blogs, forums, and social platforms. Programming status is confirmed against Onstage Entertainment Group booking records. Where the entertainment signal is small, we have said so plainly and treated it as directional rather than as a measured property-wide finding, so that the read stays honest against your own scores.
